# Keep WebinarGeek, let GoHighLevel handle the last mile

**WebinarGeek Premium, which you already pay for, is the best single platform on this checklist, and WebinarGeek as the webinar room plus GoHighLevel (GHL) for the last-minute reminders is the best combination.** I scored 19 platforms against all 20 criteria and priced a 20th (Goldcast). WebinarGeek is the only tool that's documented to cover every broadcasting and CTA item a sales webinar needs: a hybrid broadcast where a recorded video plays and then you go live, a pinned chat message, banning, a pop-up CTA, an automatic redirect to a sales page at the end, and unlimited reminders and follow-ups sent from webinargeek.com. It also has a REST API that can create registrations, which your setup already uses. Its one real gap is the 5 to 0 minute reminder, because WebinarGeek sends email in 15-minute batches. A GHL workflow with a "Wait before event" step closes that gap. Zoom Webinars, which you tested, comes 12th of 19. It has no native pop-up CTA. Its reminders are fixed at 1 week, 1 day and 1 hour before, and it sends one follow-up per audience. Running a recording and then going live needs the pricier Webinars Plus or Events license, and even then the host can't appear on video or audio while the recording plays. BigMarker, WebinarJam and Livestorm are credible fallbacks. Every verdict here comes from search extracts of vendor help pages, because the research sandbox couldn't open vendor sites directly. So treat any "?" cell and the trial checklist at the end as must-checks before you commit.

## WebinarGeek wins the room, Zoom loses on CTAs and email

The deciding criteria are the ones only some tools pass: recorded video then live in the same broadcast (#2), pop-up CTA (#9), end redirect (#10), and a reminder inside the last 15 minutes (#12). **WebinarGeek's "hybrid webinar" adds your recording as a video injection inside a live broadcast, and you start and end it manually**, so you can go on camera the moment the video finishes ([WebinarGeek hybrid webinar](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/5797206-hybrid-webinar)). Its CTA can show a pop-up or send viewers to a website, and you can set several per webinar ([WebinarGeek CTA](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/4269516-call-to-action)). The "external sales page" setting forwards viewers automatically when the webinar ends, and "Ban attendee from webinar" kicks someone out, stops them re-entering and stops them chatting ([WebinarGeek moderation](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/6592859-moderating-a-webinar)). Pinning works by hovering over a message and clicking the pin icon, but **only in live webinars, not automated ones** ([WebinarGeek chat](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/4270034-attendees-and-chat)). On email, you choose each reminder's offset, can schedule emails up to 14 days before or after, and send from noreply@webinargeek.com by default ([email scheduling](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/4269365-email-scheduling); [own sender](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/4269517-verify-your-own-email)). The catch is that **emails go out in 15-minute batches**, so a "5 minutes before" reminder can arrive up to about 15 minutes off target.

Zoom Webinars fails where it counts for a sales room. Zoom's developer forum confirms **there's no native pop-up CTA**, only SDK or third-party add-ons ([Zoom dev forum](https://devforum.zoom.us/t/call-to-action-on-zoom-webinar/10093)). Reminders are limited to 1 week, 1 day and 1 hour before, and the API can't change those timings ([Zoom community](https://community.zoom.com/webinars-19/reminder-in-webinar-2654)). Follow-ups are one to attendees and one to absentees ([Zoom community](https://community.zoom.com/webinars-19/zoom-webinar-not-sending-follow-up-email-to-attendees-absentees-876)). Simulive can switch to live automatically, but it needs Webinars Plus or Zoom Events, and **hosts can't appear on video or audio while the recording plays** ([Zoom KB0058205](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0058205)). The redirect at the end only fires if the browser launcher page stays open ([Zoom KB0070304](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0070304)). You can pin a chat message, but only one, only from desktop, and only for the current session ([Zoom KB0088614](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0088614)).

## The winning architecture: WebinarGeek room, GHL clock

Pairing WebinarGeek with GHL turns WebinarGeek's one P into a Y and leaves every other cell alone. The key design decision is **who sends which email**. Running two full reminder sets is the classic way combination setups go wrong, and re-registering someone can trigger a second vendor confirmation ([Zoom dev forum example](https://devforum.zoom.us/t/issue-with-webinar-registrants-api-list-and-adding/37651)). I recommend a split. WebinarGeek keeps the confirmation, the reminders at 1 day, 1 hour and 15 minutes, and the attendance-filtered follow-ups, all sent from webinargeek.com, so criteria 13 to 19 stay native. GHL sends only the two messages WebinarGeek can't time precisely: T-5 and "we're live" at T-0, ideally by SMS plus email.

GHL's workflow engine can do the timing. It has a **"Set Event Start Date" action, and Wait steps can count minutes, hours or days before that event** ([GHL Set Event Start Date](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/48001202723-workflow-action-set-event-start-date); [white-label Wait step doc](https://support.fgfunnels.com/article/1963-how-to-use-the-wait-step-action-in-workflows)). If the time has already passed, the step can skip ahead, so late registrants don't get stale nudges. On the WebinarGeek side, the **REST API key lives under Account > Integrations > API and can create registrations** ([WebinarGeek API](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/12058403-api)). Each registrant gets a unique watch link, which WebinarGeek's ActiveCampaign integration already syncs to the CRM ([ActiveCampaign app](https://activecampaign.com/es/apps/webinargeek-integration)). This repo also already exposes `webinargeek_register` and `webinargeek_attendance` worker tools, so most of the plumbing exists.

Here's the step-by-step flow:

| Step | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GHL form or funnel | The registrant submits. A workflow starts on "Form submitted" (or a tag being added). |
| 2 | GHL workflow | A Webhook action POSTs the contact ID, email, name and broadcast ID to your existing worker. |
| 3 | Worker to WebinarGeek API | The worker calls the register endpoint with the API key and reads back the unique watch link and the broadcast start time. Use the contact ID as an idempotency key so retries don't create duplicates. |
| 4 | Worker to GHL API | The worker writes the custom fields `webinar_watch_link` and `webinar_start_datetime` to the contact. It overwrites them for each session so links don't go stale ([Adobe on stale join links](https://experienceleaguecommunities.adobe.com/adobe-marketo-engage-27/question-on-zoom-webinar-integration-195030)). |
| 5 | GHL workflow | Set Event Start Date = `{{contact.webinar_start_datetime}}`. |
| 6 | GHL workflow | Wait 5 minutes before the event, then send an SMS and an email with `{{contact.webinar_watch_link}}`. If the time has passed, skip to the next step. |
| 7 | GHL workflow | Wait until the event time (0 minutes before), then send "We're live". |
| 8 | WebinarGeek | The room runs: video injection, then you go live, pinned link, pop-up CTA, redirect to the sales page at the end. WebinarGeek sends its own reminders and follow-ups. |
| 9 | WebinarGeek to GHL | After the broadcast, the worker pulls attendance (or Zapier's "Webinar watched" and "No Show" triggers fire ([WebinarGeek Zapier](https://help.webinargeek.com/en/articles/4273174-zapier))) and tags the contact `attended` or `no-show`. |
| 10 | GHL workflow | Optional: branch on the tag for a longer sales sequence beyond WebinarGeek's 14-day window. |

On domains: GHL's shared LC Email domain qualifies as "tool-provided". But GHL **rewrites the sending address to a msgsndr.com "plus" address**, and its own compliance guidance says the From domain should align with a branded sending domain for DMARC ([GHL sender domains](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000007249-how-sender-domains-work-in-lc-email-campaigns); [GHL Google/Yahoo compliance](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000001634-achieving-compliance-meeting-google-and-yahoo-s-email-sender-requirements-in-2024)). That's one more reason to keep the bulk of the email on WebinarGeek's sender and use GHL mainly for SMS and two short emails.

There are two alternatives. If you'd rather have GHL own every email, switch off WebinarGeek's emails and add 24h, 1h and follow-up steps to the same workflow. You get one sequence to edit, but you take on GHL's shared-domain deliverability. If Zoom is a hard requirement, Zoom plus GHL fixes all of Zoom's email failures: register through Zoom's Add Registrant API, which returns a unique join_url ([Zoom dev forum](https://devforum.zoom.us/t/webinar-invites/13811)), via Make's "Add a webinar registrant" action ([Make](https://www.make.com/en/integrations/zoom-user)) or via AEvent, which writes join links to GHL fields ([AEvent](https://aevent.com/go-high-level-integration/)). That still leaves #9 at N and #2 needing Webinars Plus. **GHL can't add a pop-up CTA to someone else's room**, so the room choice still decides the CTA criteria.

## Conclusion

The search for a better platform mostly confirms that the gaps in your current setup are about timing, not the room. The two things that separate the webinar tools least, minute-precise reminders and long follow-up sequences, are exactly what GHL's workflow engine already does better than any of them. The things GHL can't add, a hybrid video-to-live broadcast, a pop-up CTA and a redirect at the end, are what WebinarGeek already has and Zoom doesn't. So switching vendors would trade a known one-cell gap for new unknowns.

The real risk isn't features. It's two systems emailing the same person, or a stale watch link in a GHL field. Spend the effort on the worker's idempotency and on a clear rule for which system sends each email. Keep BigMarker as the documented fallback if a WebinarGeek trial check fails, and treat GHL Communities Go Live as something to watch: if it opens to non-members and adds CTAs, it could eventually remove the second vendor altogether.
