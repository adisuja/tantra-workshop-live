One pillar post a month, drafted from your Day 1 notes, tagged, published and read by clicks to page.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Publish one pillar blog post that answers a question your buyer already asks, and send its readers to your registration page. It is drafted by AI from your Day 1 notes, published in WordPress and measured in Google Analytics. You're done when the post is live, its call to action carries utm_source=blog, and a test click shows up in Google Analytics as blog traffic.

How This Changes by Mechanism

The post is dated content. Its call to action carries the session date, so refresh the date and link before each new cycle.

3Step by Step
  1. 1
    Pick One Topic From Your Buyer Persona

    A post built on a real buyer pain gets read; one built on a blank page doesn't.

    1. Open your buyer profile from Day 1 and choose one named pain point.
    2. If you have no content calendar yet, sign in to Notion (a free account works), open the Content Calendar template ↗ and add it to your workspace.
    3. Add the pain point as a new card in that calendar.
    4. Give it a title that makes a promise and has a number in it.

    Example, for a SaaS company selling route planning: "3 Reasons Your 7am Route Plan Is Dead by 9am".

    Tip If the pain point isn't in your buyer persona card, fix the card first.

  2. 2
    Have AI Draft It From Your Day 1 Notes

    AI given your notes writes about your buyer; AI given one line writes filler.

    1. Give Claude Code (or any AI) your Day 1 outputs (buyer persona card, offer, method and objections) and the prompt below.
    2. Check every claim traces back to those notes.
    3. Delete anything it made up, especially numbers, and anything marked [NEEDS SOURCE] that you can't source.

  3. 3
    Lay the Post Out in Your Blog Editor

    Most readers are on a phone, so structure does the work.

    1. In WordPress, open Posts → Add New Post and paste the draft.
    2. Use one H1 that states the promise, a subheading per idea, one worked example and one call to action near the end.
    3. In the sidebar's Post tab, click Set featured image and add one. Keep paragraphs short.
  4. 4
    Fill Out the Search Settings

    Search settings decide how the post appears in Google. An SEO plugin such as Yoast shows the fields and a traffic-light check.

    1. If Yoast SEO isn't installed, open Plugins → Add Plugin in WordPress, search for Yoast SEO, click Install Now, then Activate.
    2. Focus keyphrase (in the Yoast SEO sidebar): the words your buyer types.
    3. Click Search appearance, then fill in the Slug: the short web address, in plain words.
    4. Meta description (same panel): one sentence that makes the promise.
  5. 5
    Tag the Call-to-Action Link

    An untagged link can't be traced to a registration, so the channel can't be judged.

    1. Open Google's Campaign URL Builder ↗.
    2. Paste your registration page address into website URL. The field is required.
    3. Enter source blog, medium organic and campaign {event_slug}.
    4. Paste the finished link into the call to action.
  6. 6
    Publish, Then Read It Like a Stranger

    Once published, the post is public and search engines can pick it up almost at once.

    1. Click Publish, then Publish again in the panel that asks if you are ready to publish.
    2. Open the live page on your phone.
    3. Check the date is right, the call-to-action link opens your registration page with the tag intact, and no placeholders are left.

  7. 7
    Read Clicks to Page Every Week

    Engagement isn't traffic. Clicks to your registration page tell you if the post sells the session. GA4 must already be on these pages (Stage 2 guide, step 7).

    1. In Google Analytics, open Reports → Engagement → Pages and screens and note the post's views for the week. The Reports menu differs by property, so the report may sit under another collection.
    2. Open Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition. Change the table's first column from the channel group to Session source / medium, then type blog in the search box above the table.
    3. Click the + next to the column name and add Page path and screen class. Note the sessions that reached your registration page.
    4. Divide step 3 by step 1. The playbook gate: 3% or more, keep going; under 1%, change the call to action before anything else.
    5. Hand the post to the Social guide to cut into clips and quotes, and use it as that month's newsletter issue.

    Read by Session source / medium, not by channel group. GA4's channel groups file medium organic under Organic Search, so the blog has no row of its own there.

Draft the Pillar Post
Using my Day 1 notes (buyer persona card, offer, method, objections), draft a pillar blog post that answers this question my buyer is asking: [question]. Structure: a promise headline with a number, the problem in the buyer's words, three to five sections with subheadings (one idea each), one worked example from my notes, then an invite to my session on [date] with this link: [tagged link]. Use only facts and numbers that appear in my notes; if you need one that isn't there, write [NEEDS SOURCE]. Short paragraphs, plain words, no dashes.
