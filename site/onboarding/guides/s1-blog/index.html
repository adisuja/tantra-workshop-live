Blog

One pillar post a month, drafted from your Day 1 notes, published on your funnel, read by clicks to page.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Publish one pillar blog post that answers a question your buyer already asks, and send its readers to your registration page. It is drafted by AI from your Day 1 notes, published by the agent on your own funnel (no blog platform to sign up for) and measured in Google Analytics. You're done when the post is live at /blog on your funnel, its call to action carries utm_source=blog, and a test click shows up in Google Analytics as blog traffic.

How This Changes by Mechanism

The post is dated content. Its call to action carries the session date, so refresh the date and link before each new cycle.

3Step by Step
  1. 1
    Pick One Topic From Your Buyer Persona

    A post built on a real buyer pain gets read; one built on a blank page doesn't.

    1. Open your buyer profile from Day 1 and choose one named pain point.
    2. If you have no content calendar yet, make one in Google Sheets: a row per post with the date, the pain point, the title and a status column.
    3. Add the pain point as a new row.
    4. Give it a title that makes a promise and has a number in it.

    Example, for a SaaS company selling route planning: "3 Reasons Your 7am Route Plan Is Dead by 9am".

    Tip If the pain point isn't in your buyer persona card, fix the card first.

    Capture Needed

    A real screenshot goes here. Until then, follow the words above.

  2. 2
    Have AI Draft It From Your Day 1 Notes

    AI given your notes writes about your buyer; AI given one line writes filler.

    1. Give Claude Code (or any AI) your Day 1 outputs (buyer persona card, offer, method and objections) and the prompt below.
    2. Check every claim traces back to those notes.
    3. Delete anything it made up, especially numbers, and anything marked [NEEDS SOURCE] that you can't source.
    Capture Needed

    Claude Code terminal from a real operator session: the Draft the Pillar Post prompt pasted in, the Day 1 notes files it read, and the draft file it wrote.

    A real screenshot goes here. Until then, follow the words above.

  3. 3
    Approve It and the Agent Lays It Out

    Most readers are on a phone, so structure does the work. Your pillar post is the first post on the Approve your posts card, and once you approve it the agent publishes it on your own funnel at /blog/<slug>. There is no blog platform to sign up for.

    1. Start the post with one line that states the promise. It becomes the page title.
    2. Use a ## subheading per idea, one worked example and one call to action near the end, written as [REGISTRATION LINK].
    3. Keep paragraphs short. Lists stay lists on the page.
    Capture Needed

    The Approve your posts card with the pillar post as the first post, and the same post live at /blog/<slug> on a funnel.

    A real screenshot goes here. Until then, follow the words above.

  4. 4
    Check the Search Settings

    Search settings decide how the post appears in Google. The agent writes them from the post, so you only check them.

    1. Slug: the short web address, made from your title in plain words. Change the title if the slug reads badly.
    2. Title: your first line, as written.
    3. Meta description: your first paragraph, cut to about 155 characters. Make that paragraph the promise in one sentence.
    Capture Needed

    View source of a live /blog/<slug> page showing the title and meta description tags.

    A real screenshot goes here. Until then, follow the words above.

  5. 5
    Tag the Call-to-Action Link

    An untagged link can't be traced to a registration, so the channel can't be judged. The agent turns [REGISTRATION LINK] into your page with source blog, medium organic and the post's slug as the campaign. Tag any other link you add yourself:

    1. Open Google's Campaign URL Builder ↗.
    2. Paste your registration page address into website URL. The field is required.
    3. Enter source blog, medium organic and campaign {event_slug}.
    4. Paste the finished link into the call to action.
  6. 6
    Publish, Then Read It Like a Stranger

    Once published, the post is public and search engines can pick it up almost at once.

    1. Approve the post on the Approve your posts card. The agent puts it live with your next funnel update.
    2. Open https://<your funnel>/blog/<slug> on your phone.
    3. Check the date is right, the call-to-action link opens your registration page with the tag intact, and no placeholders are left.
    Capture Needed

    A live published pillar post on a phone-width screen with the call-to-action block visible.

    A real screenshot goes here. Until then, follow the words above.

  7. 7
    Read Clicks to Page Every Week

    Engagement isn't traffic. Clicks to your registration page tell you if the post sells the session. GA4 must already be on these pages (Stage 2 guide, step 7).

    1. In Google Analytics, open Reports → Engagement → Pages and screens and note the post's views for the week. The Reports menu differs by property, so the report may sit under another collection.
    2. Open Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition. Change the table's first column from the channel group to Session source / medium, then type blog in the search box above the table.
    3. Click the + next to the column name and add Page path and screen class. Note the sessions that reached your registration page.
    4. Divide step 3 by step 1. The playbook gate: 3% or more, keep going; under 1%, change the call to action before anything else.
    5. Hand the post to the Social guide to cut into clips and quotes, and use it as that month's newsletter issue.

    Read by Session source / medium, not by channel group. GA4's channel groups file medium organic under Organic Search, so the blog has no row of its own there.

Draft the Pillar Post
Using my Day 1 notes (buyer persona card, offer, method, objections), draft a pillar blog post that answers this question my buyer is asking: [question]. Structure: a promise headline with a number, the problem in the buyer's words, three to five sections with subheadings (one idea each), one worked example from my notes, then an invite to my session on [date] with the link written as [REGISTRATION LINK]. Put the headline alone on the first line and start each subheading with ##. Use only facts and numbers that appear in my notes; if you need one that isn't there, write [NEEDS SOURCE]. Short paragraphs, plain words, no dashes.
