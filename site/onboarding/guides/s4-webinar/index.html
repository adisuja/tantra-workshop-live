The Live Webinar

Set up the room, rehearse it, run sixty minutes on a clock, and close while people are still watching.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Set up one live webinar room, staff it with named roles, rehearse it, then run sixty minutes on a fixed clock: 45 minutes of teaching, 15 of Q&A, and the offer landing once near the end. Every registration, attendance and poll answer flows into your CRM as a tag. Done means the dry run ran end to end, the offer button appears when your moderator starts it at its minute, and a test registration arrives in the CRM tagged webinar registrant.

  1. 1
    Create the Webinar Room

    The Centre Does This

    The event-setup job creates and schedules the WebinarGeek broadcast for you, or, if you use Zoom, a Zoom Pro meeting with registration on and the room set up like a webinar.

    You Do This

    Give the centre your WebinarGeek key and webinar ID (or Zoom app credentials).

    Building it by hand instead

    In your webinar tool (WebinarGeek in this stack; the free trial runs 14 days, and presenters need the Chrome browser), click + New webinar and choose Present live (everyone joins at the same time). Give it the same title, date and time as your registration page, and set the length to 60 minutes.

    • Pick the Webinar format, so attendees watch and presenters are on stage.
    • The time shown is in your account's time zone (Account → My account → Settings). Registrants see their own local time on the page, in emails and in reminders.
    • Leave replays switched on; you'll set their expiry in step 12.
    • When the pages and emails are ready, open Publish in the left sidebar and click Publish. The registration link is not active until then.

    Tip Zoom works too, on a Pro plan or higher, with no Webinars add-on: a Pro meeting holds 100 people (you and your co-hosts count) and gives every registrant a personal link. At the start, open Security and untick Unmute Themselves and Start Video, and make co-hosts in the meeting. Zoom sends no reminder emails for meetings, so your funnel sends them.

  2. 2
    Ask Only for the Fields You Will Use

    The Centre Does This

    The funnel's registration form registers each sign-up on WebinarGeek for you.

    Building it by hand instead

    Registration fields decide what you can do later. Use the playbook's five, and nothing more:

    1. First name
    2. Last name
    3. Business email
    4. Profile link (so the team can follow up personally)
    5. Phone, only if you will call or text

    If the form shows only first name and email, add the rest:

    1. Click the webinar, then Edit webinar.
    2. Open Registration → Registration fields.
    3. Click Add general field for last name and phone number.
    4. Click Add custom field and pick a text field for the profile link. Custom fields are a text field, a dropdown or radio buttons.

    If people register on your own page (the Tantra Funnel), send those fields to the webinar tool, so there's one list and one join link per person. The routes are the embed form, the API (the key is under Account → Integrations → API), Zapier or a CSV import.

    Tip Every extra field costs registrations. Ask the rest after they've said yes.

  3. 3
    Send Every Event to Your CRM

    The Centre Does This

    The event-setup job creates the account-wide webhook for you, and the funnel turns each event into a tag and a branch.

    Building it by hand instead

    Send each event to your CRM or automation endpoint with a webhook:

    1. Open Account → Integrations → Webhooks.
    2. Enter a Custom label and the incoming-webhook URL of your sync workflow. Only HTTPS URLs are accepted.
    3. Click Add trigger and enable these eight WebinarGeek triggers on it, each set to send information about a webinar subscriber and active for all webinars: New registration, Webinar ended, New live viewer, New replay viewer, New poll vote, New call to action, Unsubscribed from webinar and New viewer.

    The sync turns them into the tags Stages 3 and 5 key off:

    • New registration → webinar registrant
    • New live viewer → webinar attended and live webinar watched
    • Webinar ended, for anyone with no live view → live webinar not watched
    • New replay viewer → webinar attended and watched webinar replay
    • Unsubscribed from webinar → webinar unsubscribed
    • New poll vote → the poll answer

    The webhook only sends the event. The rule that turns it into a tag lives in your CRM or automation tool.

    Set this up before registration opens: triggers do not run for events that already happened. New live viewer and New replay viewer arrive 1 to 2 hours after the session. That is normal, and it is why every after-the-session branch waits at least 2 hours.

    The webinar tool is the only system that knows who really watched, so this step is what makes every follow-up personal.

  4. 4
    Put Five Names on Five Roles

    The host only teaches. Four more people run the room, and each role gets a real name before the day:

    1. Host: teaches and makes the offer.
    2. Content moderator: watches the clock and relays questions.
    3. Tech moderator: stage, screens, anyone who can't hear.
    4. Chat and Q&A: posts the link, clears spam, marks answered questions.
    5. DM operator: sends the 1:1 messages at minutes 3, 15 and 40.

    Add them as co-presenters or moderators before the day:

    1. In the webinar overview, click the webinar's title.
    2. Scroll to Webinar team and click Add team member.
    3. Enter the person's details and choose the role Co-presenter or Moderator.
    4. Click Save.

    On the day, you can also invite someone from the studio: Attendance tab → Invite team member. Moderators are not included in the Basic plan, and the number of co-presenters depends on your plan. Keep the Team chat tab open backstage.

    Tip A role without a name on it doesn't get done. One person can cover two roles; nobody covers three.

  5. 5
    Build the Four Polls Before the Day

    Four polls, in this order, each doing one job:

    1. Persona: on slide 1. Who's in the room.
    2. Challenge: early. The main problem, in their words.
    3. Investment priority: around minute 10 to 15. Separates browsers from buyers.
    4. Discovery-call interest: on the pitch slide. This is the list your close follows up.

    Create all four now, so launching one live is a single click: Edit webinar → Interactions → Create interaction → Poll. In a live webinar a poll does not start by itself. Someone opens the Interactions tab in the studio and clicks Start.

  6. 6
    Add the Offer Button and Set Its Minute

    The Centre Does This

    The close-setup job creates your booking event (a Cal.com Discovery call) and your checkout (Dodo or Stripe) with their webhooks, so you have both links ready.

    You Do This

    Choose the price, the currency and the discovery questions, and say an explicit yes before live payments are switched on.

    Building it by hand instead

    Have the link ready first: your booking link (offer about $1,000 or more) or your checkout link (under that). In Cal.com, a booking link comes from Event types → New event type. In Dodo Payments, a checkout link comes from a product, and live payments only work once your account verification is approved.

    Then build the button:

    1. Open Edit webinar → Interactions → Create interaction → Call to action.
    2. Under Button action, choose Open a url/website and paste the link. Add ?src=live to it, so the sale is credited to the session.
    3. Set Default duration long enough to reach the end of the hour.

    In a live webinar the button does not appear by itself; start times only work for automated and on-demand webinars. When your offer slide appears, the tech moderator opens the Interactions tab in the studio and clicks Start. To keep the button up until you close it, choose End manually when starting it.

    Tip The offer lands once. Don't show the button during the teaching.

  7. 7
    Open Q&A With Upvotes

    Open Edit webinar → Webinar → Q&A and set it to Public, so attendees can post, see and upvote each other's questions. Answer the most-voted first. If the room is quiet, the host answers three to five common questions openly, as questions people often ask. Never post questions under invented attendee names.

  8. 8
    Turn Recording On

    Live webinars are recorded by default, and recording starts when you start the webinar. Check the switch is still on before the day: click the webinar, then Edit webinar → Webinar → Settings → Enable recordings for live webinars. The recording becomes your replay, your no-show recovery and your next month's clips. Say "this is recorded" out loud during the session.

  9. 9
    Run a Full Dry Run Two to Three Days Before

    Same computer, same network, same room, same slides. In the webinar overview, pick the webinar and click Dry run, then enter the room and click Start dry run. Walk the whole hour:

    • Every presenter reaches the stage, with the right name showing.
    • A test attendee joins on a second device, to see what they see. In the Attendance tab, click the eye icon to open the attendee view, or Invite test attendee to send a link.
    • Each poll starts, and the offer button appears when the moderator starts it at its minute.
    • Hand-offs between roles are agreed out loud.

    A dry run is not recorded. To test the recording, run a short real live webinar that only your team registers for.

    On the morning, make a second person co-presenter as a fallback.

    Tip Every failure you find in the dry run costs nothing. The same failure live costs the room.

  10. 10
    Run the Hour on the Clock

    Enter the room with Open live webinar. On the hour, click Start live webinar, confirm, then click Go live! The clock runs the session, not the mood in the room:

    1. 0 to 5: doors open; ask where people are joining from.
    2. 5 to 10: who this is for, why to stay to the end, poll 1.
    3. 10 to 45: teaching; poll 2 early, poll 3 around minute 10 to 15.
    4. Near 45: the offer, once; poll 4 on the pitch slide.
    5. 45 to 60: Q&A by upvotes, then close by repeating the offer.

    Each poll starts when someone clicks Start in the Interactions tab. Meanwhile, chat posts the link every 5 minutes after the offer, and the DM operator messages at minutes 3, 15 and 40.

  11. 11
    Close While They Are Still in the Room

    The Centre Does This

    A booking sets the contact to Eval Call Scheduled, a payment sets purchased and sends the purchase email, and attendees get the offer follow-up at the end of the session without anyone sending it.

    You Do This

    Start the offer button at the offer minute.

    Building it by hand instead

    At the offer minute the tech moderator starts the button, and it appears on stage for everyone. Then:

    • Anyone who answered yes to poll 4 gets a personal message with the booking link within the hour.
    • The link stays pinned in chat until the end.
    • The deadline you name is real, and it's the same one on the slide and in every email.
  12. 12
    Give the Replay a Real Expiry

    Click the webinar, go to the broadcast you just ran, then open Replay → Settings. Turn on Availability and set the date and time the replay closes. The setting applies to that broadcast only. Then keep to that date. People who missed it get the replay 24 hours later, and the Tantra Flow tells them when it closes.

  13. 13
    Export the Reports and Tag Everyone

    The Centre Does This

    The WebinarGeek webhook tags attended, replay watched and no-show live, so there is no CSV to import. The funnel falls back to no-show three hours after the end if no feed arrives.

    Building it by hand instead

    About two hours after the session ends, open the webinar's Statistics and export three reports:

    1. Registrations tab → Export to CSV: registrants.
    2. Viewers tab → Export to CSV: attendees, with when and how long each one watched.
    3. Interactions tab → View results → Export to CSV: poll answers per person.

    Check the CRM has the same people tagged webinar attended and live webinar not watched, and add each person's poll 4 answer. Those two tags can take up to two hours to arrive, so don't check straight after the hour.

    These tags start the attended branch and the no-show branch of the Tantra Flow, and the Stage 5 close chain.

  14. 14
    Copy the Webinar for the Next Run

    Copy this webinar, emails, interactions and presentation included, and only change the date: in the webinar overview, click the arrow next to View details, then Duplicate webinar, then Create webinar. Each new run then takes minutes, not a rebuild. To repeat the exact same webinar and keep its registration link, WebinarGeek suggests adding a date instead: Edit webinar → General → Dates and times → + Add an extra broadcast. Move the old event page's date to the new one before the current session starts, so its listing keeps its attendees.

Webinar Framework: How to Use Salesforce AI to Cut Down Customer Service Costs by 70% in 2026

Approach 1: Process Optimization (Augmentation Model)

What It Means:
This approach enhances existing workflows without fundamentally changing how support operates. AI acts as an intelligent assistant that accelerates human work, making agents faster, more consistent, and more effective.
How It Works:

  • AI assists human agents in real-time during customer interactions
  • Systems retrieve relevant information, suggest responses, and summarize context
  • Humans remain in control of all decisions and final outputs
  • Current processes stay largely intact; speed and accuracy improve

Typical Use Cases:

  • Intelligent response drafting: AI suggests contextual replies based on ticket history and knowledge base
  • Automated case summarization: Converting long conversation threads into digestible summaries
  • Smart information retrieval: Surfacing relevant help articles, previous tickets, or account details instantly
  • Assisted troubleshooting: Guiding agents through diagnostic steps with contextual prompts
  • Example: Intelligent shipment tracking with human review before customer response

Cost Reduction Mechanism:
Reduce Average Handle Time (AHT) by accelerating information access and reducing manual writing. Lower training time for new agents through embedded guidance systems.
Risk Profile: Low
Human oversight remains constant. Errors are caught before reaching customers. Rollback is straightforward if issues arise.
Best Starting Point For:

  • Organizations new to AI in customer service
  • Highly regulated industries requiring human oversight
  • Teams with complex, judgment-heavy support scenarios
  • Companies without mature data infrastructure
Approach 2: Process Reimagination (Automation Model)

What It Means:
This approach redesigns workflows from the ground up, eliminating entire categories of human work. AI doesn't just assist: it independently handles complete processes end-to-end.
How It Works:

  • AI systems operate autonomously within defined boundaries
  • Certain ticket types or request categories are fully automated
  • Humans are removed from routine workflows entirely
  • Escalation to humans occurs only when confidence thresholds aren't met

Typical Use Cases:

  • Autonomous policy verification: Checking eligibility, coverage, or compliance without human review
  • Self-service order modifications: Processing returns, cancellations, or changes automatically
  • Automated account provisioning: Creating accounts, setting permissions, sending credentials
  • Multi-step approval workflows: Routing requests through validation chains without manual touchpoints
  • Example: Automated Smart Analysis, where complex policy checks, approvals, or multi-step analyses happen in the background

Cost Reduction Mechanism:
Eliminate high percentages of routine ticket volume by fully automating high-frequency, low-complexity requests. Redirect human agents exclusively to complex, high-value interactions.
Risk Profile: Medium to High
Requires robust data quality, clear decision logic, and strong governance. Mistakes directly impact customers. Requires sophisticated monitoring and fallback systems.
Best Starting Point For:

  • Organizations with mature data infrastructure
  • High-volume, repetitive support workflows
  • Companies with clear, rule-based processes
  • Teams ready for organizational change management
Part 1: The Progressive Adoption Framework

Why Traditional Support Models Fail to Scale Cost-Effectively
Most support organizations operate on a fundamentally linear model:

  • More volume = More agents = More cost
  • Agent productivity plateaus after initial training
  • Knowledge fragmentation leads to inconsistent quality
  • Manual processes create bottlenecks that can't be optimized away

The result: support costs scale in direct proportion to growth, consuming increasing percentages of revenue over time.
The AI-Driven Alternative: The Agent Maturity Ladder
Rather than choosing between full automation and no automation, successful organizations follow a progressive maturity path:
AI Providing Information → AI Making Decisions → AI Taking Autonomous Actions
Level 1: AI Providing Information
AI retrieves and surfaces relevant information to human agents. Agents make all decisions and take all actions.
Role: Information assistant
Level 2: AI Making Decisions
AI analyzes situations, evaluates options, and recommends specific actions. Humans review recommendations and choose whether to act.
Role: Decision advisor
Level 3: AI Taking Autonomous Actions
AI independently executes complete workflows within defined boundaries. Humans monitor exceptions and handle escalations only.
Role: Autonomous operator
Strategic Insight: Build Trust Before Scaling
Organizations that achieve significant cost reduction don't jump directly to autonomous actions. They:
1. Begin with information assistance to build confidence and gather operational data
2. Progress to decision support as trust and accuracy improve
3. Expand to autonomous actions only for specific, well-understood use cases
4. Scale systematically based on performance data and user feedback
5. Continuously optimize across all maturity levels
Key Principle:
Transitioning from "outputs" to "actions" requires building trust at each level before progressing.

Part 3: The 4-Step Implementation Methodology

This framework outlines how to build an autonomous support operation within the Salesforce ecosystem.

Step 1: Identifying High-ROI Use Cases

Objective: Find the right starting point, avoid "big bang" rollouts that create excessive risk.
The Use Case Selection Framework:
Use an Impact vs. Feasibility Matrix to evaluate opportunities systematically.
Impact Assessment: Prioritize tasks that align with:

  • Cost reduction: Which use cases eliminate the most manual work?
  • Volume management: Which handles the highest ticket volumes?

Feasibility Assessment: Evaluate based on:

  • Data quality: Is the necessary data accurate, complete, and accessible?
  • Existing API connectivity: Can AI access required systems without major integration work?

Target Zone: Focus on opportunities in the "quick-win" quadrant: high impact on costs and volume, combined with strong data quality and API readiness.

Data-Backed Prioritization

When multiple use cases appear equally promising or stakeholder preferences conflict:
Incorporate quantitative evidence:

  • Current process volume
  • Average handling time
  • Error rates
  • Customer satisfaction scores
  • Resolution rates
  • Revenue leakage metrics

Purpose:
Historical performance data helps objectively rank opportunities, resolve disagreements, and select the highest-ROI starting point.

Strategic Implementation Approach

Start Small, Prove Value, Then Expand:
1. Begin with assistive and semi-autonomous use cases
2. Focus on opportunities that are high-impact, low-to-medium effort, and low-risk
3. Target use cases where AI augments human work without requiring full autonomy from day one
4. Deliver measurable ROI rapidly (often in weeks to months)
5. Build internal confidence and minimize risks around adoption, compliance, or integration
6. Use early success as foundation for expanding to more complex workflows
This approach:

  • Avoids common pitfalls like over-ambitious projects or unclear value
  • Enables a scalable path from pilots to enterprise-wide transformation
  • Reduces risks around adoption, compliance, and integration
Step 2: Designing the Reasoning Logic

Core Principle:
Leaders must decide how the agent "thinks" before it acts.
Fundamental Question:
Not every agent needs reasoning, but every agent needs clarity about its decision-making approach.
Determine:

  • Is this a reactive agent (retrieve, summarize, respond)?
  • Or a decision-making agent that plans, evaluates, or chooses?

This leads to two key design choices:

Choice 1: Deterministic Logic vs. Probabilistic Judgment

Deterministic Logic
Probabilistic Judgment
Fixed rules for specific outcomes
Model-driven inference
Same input → same outcome
Likelihood-based outcomes
Best for: Compliance checks, specific approvals, validations, routing

Implementation Guidance:
Use Deterministic Logic when:

  • Regulatory requirements mandate specific workflows
  • Financial transactions require zero-tolerance accuracy
  • Compliance checks must be fully auditable
  • Approval processes follow explicit organizational rules

Use Probabilistic Judgment when:

  • Understanding customer requests from free-text input
  • Personalizing responses based on context and history
  • Recommending next-best actions
  • Interpreting ambiguous situations

Common Production Pattern:
Most production agents blend both approaches:

  • Deterministic rules decide when to act
  • Probabilistic models decide how to respond

  • Latency-sensitive vs. depth-sensitive use cases

Step 3: Automated Validation and Testing

Core Reality:
Manual testing is no longer sufficient for dynamic AI.

Why Traditional Testing Fails for AI

As AI agents move beyond static workflows and operate across dynamic, conversational paths, testing becomes exponentially more complex.
The Complexity Problem:
Agentic AI workflows:

  • Can execute the same task in multiple valid ways
  • Trigger different paths based on context, data, or user input
  • Interact dynamically with APIs, systems, and policies

At scale, this results in:

  • A vast number of workflow permutations
  • Variations driven by user demographics, languages, and behaviors
  • Edge cases that surface only in real-world usage

The Limitation:
Traditional testing approaches struggle to keep up. It becomes nearly impossible to predefine and validate every scenario using static test cases or manual scripts. Manual testing becomes impractical for Agentic AI at scale.

The Solution: AI-Testing-AI Methodology

Approach:
Use AI-driven testing: AI systems designed specifically to test and validate other AI agents.
How It Works:
1. Synthetic Data Generation:

  • AI generates realistic customer interactions automatically
  • Creates hundreds or thousands of test scenarios in real time
  • Covers positive scenarios, negative cases, and edge conditions

2. Automated Workflow Testing:

  • Stress-test agents across all scenario types
  • Test different conversation paths and decision branches
  • Validate behavior under various conditions

3. Validation Against Human Baselines:

  • Compare AI agent responses to historical human agent outcomes
  • Ensure AI performs at human-comparable levels
  • Identify gaps where AI underperforms

4. Scale Measurement:

  • Measure accuracy, reliability, and consistency at scale
  • Track success rates across thousands of test interactions
  • Validate integration robustness
Testing Scope

Using standard testing platforms and frameworks:

  • Validate agent behavior across predefined scenarios and edge cases
  • Test consistency and failure handling
  • Ensure the agent's "Actions" stage matches organizational standards
  • Verify integration robustness across systems
  • Confirm agents meet baseline functional and performance expectations

Outcome:
This combined approach ensures agents don't just work technically, but perform at a human-comparable level in real operational conditions.

Step 4: Phased Deployment and Adoption

Core Principle:
Adoption is not automatic. Transitioning from "outputs" to "actions" requires building trust.

Rollout Strategy

Requirement:
AI requires phased activation, not big-bang releases.
Implementation Approach:

  • Controlled rollout by use case or user group
  • Clear fallback and escalation paths
  • Defined ownership for agent behavior

Progressive Rollout Pattern:
1. Start with internal testing (small group of support agents)
2. Pilot with low-risk customer segments
3. Monitor closely and iterate
4. Expand gradually based on performance data
5. Maintain fallback paths throughout

Training: Teaching Trust and Override

Focus Areas:

  • Video-based, role-specific training: Practical demonstrations of AI in real scenarios
  • When to trust the AI: What types of requests is AI highly reliable on?
  • When to override: When should agents intervene or correct AI outputs?
  • When to escalate: What scenarios require human judgment or supervisor involvement?
  • Setting the right expectations: AI as a co-worker, not a replacement

Key Principle:
Users must understand both capabilities and limitations to use AI effectively.

The Maturity Ladder in Practice

Level 1: AI Providing Information

  • Deploy AI to assist agents with information retrieval
  • Agents review all AI-provided information before acting
  • Build confidence in data accuracy and relevance

Level 2: AI Making Decisions

  • AI begins recommending specific actions
  • Agents review recommendations and choose whether to proceed
  • Trust increases as recommendation quality proves consistent

Level 3: AI Taking Autonomous Actions

  • AI handles specific use cases end-to-end
  • Agents monitor exceptions and handle escalations only
  • Full autonomy achieved for defined workflows

Movement Between Levels:
Progress only when performance data and user confidence support the transition.

Part 4: Operational Guardrails

To maintain safety while cutting costs, the following must be integrated into the architecture:

Guardrail 1: Security & Compliance

Requirements:

  • PII Masking: Personally Identifiable Information must be masked or redacted
  • Role-based access: What the agent can see, say, and do must be controlled by user role
  • User data protection: Customer interactions are not stored or used for model training
  • Auditability: All actions and decisions must be traceable

Especially Critical In:

  • Financial services
  • Healthcare & life sciences
  • Insurance
  • Telecom
  • Public sector and regulated enterprises

Key Principle:
Security and trust are features, not constraints.

Guardrail 2: Confidence Thresholds

Core Requirement:
The system must know when it is "unsure" and trigger a context-rich handoff to a human.
Implementation:

  • Define confidence threshold levels (e.g., <80% confidence = escalate)
  • When AI uncertainty is detected, escalate gracefully
  • Provide complete context to the human taking over:
  • Full conversation history
  • Attempted actions
  • Why AI couldn't resolve
  • Relevant customer data

Key Principle:
Clear signaling that escalation is intentional, not an error.

Guardrail 3: Fallbacks & Human Handoffs

Plan Upfront:

  • Clean, context-rich handoffs to humans
  • Confidence thresholds for escalation
  • Clear signaling to both agents and customers

Design Principle:
Well-designed agents know when to step aside.

Guardrail 4: Performance & Scale Requirements

Design For:

  • Response-time expectations
  • Concurrent usage and peak loads
  • Cost predictability
  • Dependency failures across models, APIs, or data sources

Key Takeaway:
Production agents must be resilient, not just intelligent.

Guardrail 5: Prompt and Model Governance

Requirements:

  • Versioning of all instructions and prompts
  • Approval processes for changes
  • Rollback capability when issues arise
  • Clear ownership of agent behavior

Why This Matters:
Prevents unauthorized changes and enables rapid recovery from problems.

Part 6: Common Implementation Patterns Across Industries

Recurring Problem Patterns
Observation:
Industries change. Service problems don't.
When stripped of domain language and organizational structure, the same service patterns repeat across sectors, healthcare, manufacturing, logistics, education, consumer services, financial services, and more.
Common Patterns:
Pattern 1: Status Inquiries

  • Enquiries that boil down to checking status
  • High volume, low complexity
  • Clear data sources available
  • Ideal for autonomous handling

Pattern 2: Search vs. Resolution

  • Agents spending time searching for information instead of resolving issues
  • Knowledge fragmentation across systems
  • Opportunity for AI-assisted information retrieval

Pattern 3: Field Context Needs

  • Field teams needing fast context and frictionless logging
  • Mobile access requirements
  • Real-time data synchronization needs

Pattern 4: Volume Without Signal

  • Managers overwhelmed by volume but starved of actionable signal
  • Need for intelligent summarization and prioritization
  • Analytics to surface patterns and trends

Strategic Implication:
Solutions can be designed as reusable templates that apply across different industries with appropriate customization.
