LinkedIn Outreach

Connect, follow up and invite through HeyReach at a safe pace, plus a company event page.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Reach named buyers through your own LinkedIn profile without risking the account, and publish a company event that links to your registration page. It runs on HeyReach; people register on your own page. You're done when the campaign runs at a safe pace, every link carries utm_source=linkedin and LinkedIn registrations show in your CRM with that source.

3Step by Step
  1. 1
    Connect Your LinkedIn Account to HeyReach

    HeyReach sends from your own LinkedIn session. It also offers a password login; use the cookie method instead, so your password stays with you.

    Before you start, you need:

    • A HeyReach account. The free trial runs 14 days and needs no card. Each LinkedIn account takes one seat.
    • A LinkedIn profile with at least 100 connections. HeyReach says to grow a new profile to 100 by hand before connecting it.
    • Google Chrome. The cookie method only works there.
    • No other LinkedIn automation tool running on the same profile.
    1. In HeyReach, open LinkedIn Accounts in the left sidebar and click Connect account.
    2. Choose Connect with cookies, not Credentials login.
    3. Install the Cookie-Editor extension from the link HeyReach shows, then log in to LinkedIn in the same browser.
    4. Open the extension on the LinkedIn tab and let it read LinkedIn's cookies. Click Export and choose JSON.
    5. Paste the result into the box in HeyReach and click Next.
    6. When HeyReach shows your profile, click Yes, that's me.
    7. Pick an inbox privacy option and the proxy location closest to where you really are, then click Connect account. Syncing can take up to 10 minutes.

    The account is ready when its status reads Available. If you log out of LinkedIn on that computer, the session ends and you repeat these steps.

  2. 2
    Set Your Daily Limits

    LinkedIn restricts accounts that send too fast, and it does not publish its exact limits. Start low and raise slowly.

    1. Open LinkedIn Accounts and click Configure limits on the account's row.
    2. Start connection requests low, about 15 to 20 a day, and keep HeyReach's suggested defaults for messages and InMail.
    3. Click Save settings.
    4. Raise requests a little each week only while acceptance stays healthy and LinkedIn shows no warnings.

    Limits apply per account across every campaign, not per campaign. HeyReach itself stops at 40 connection requests a day and 200 a week per account.

  3. 3
    Import One Clean List

    A campaign's lead list cannot be changed once the campaign is launched. Clean first.

    1. Remove customers, competitors and anyone on your do-not-contact list from the CSV.
    2. Check that every row has three things: LinkedIn profile URL, first name and last name. HeyReach skips any row missing one of them.
    3. Open Leads in the left sidebar and click Add leads.
    4. Choose Import from CSV and click Continue.
    5. Upload the file and map each column. Map the company column too, because your messages use {COMPANY}.
    6. Under Select a list to import to, pick a list or click Create empty list, then click Import.

    Example: for a SaaS company, one list of Heads of Operations from the Day 1 prospect list, customers removed.

  4. 4
    Create One Campaign per Voice

    One campaign can't change its wording per sender, so each sender gets their own campaign and list.

    1. Open Campaigns and click Start a new Campaign.
    2. Put the sender's name in the campaign name and click Create.
    3. On the Leads tab, select that sender's list. No prospect sits on two lists.
    4. Under Exclude options, tick Exclude leads contacted from other HeyReach campaigns and Exclude leads on your blacklist.
    5. Click Continue.
  5. 5
    Build the Sequence With an Accepted Branch

    Only people who accept your request should get messages. Everyone else is left alone.

    1. On the Sequence tab, choose to build from scratch. Click the plus sign and add Send connection request. HeyReach adds both branches itself: Accepted on the right, Not accepted yet on the left.
    2. Click the connection request step and write the note. It holds 300 characters.
    3. Fill in the Fallback message. HeyReach sends it when a lead has no value for a variable. Click Save, or the step keeps none of it.
    4. On the Accepted side, click the plus sign and add Send message steps at 0, 3, 7, 12 and 25 days after acceptance.
    5. Use only {FIRST_NAME} and {COMPANY}, typed exactly like that. Any other spelling is sent as raw text. Every link carries utm_source=linkedin.

    A free LinkedIn account can add a note to only about 5 to 10 requests a month. After that, HeyReach sends the request without the note.

    Example note for a SaaS company: "Sam, saw your team opened a third depot, congrats. I work with Heads of Operations on what the morning replan really costs. Worth connecting?"

  6. 6
    Set the Not-Accepted Wait in Days

    This wait is how long someone has to accept. Set in hours, a person who accepts on day two never gets a message.

    1. On the Not accepted yet branch, click the pencil on the wait and set it in days. HeyReach recommends 7 to 12 days.
    2. Click the Send connection request step and check Withdraw connection request at the top of the panel (the second image in step 5), then click Save. If invites withdraw at day 14, the window ends at day 14 whatever the branch says.

    Once a request is withdrawn, LinkedIn blocks a new request to that person for three weeks.

  7. 7
    Pick the Senders and Re-Check Them

    The wrong sender puts the wrong voice on every message.

    1. On the LinkedIn Senders tab, tick the sending account.
    2. Click the Set Limits icon on its row and confirm the daily caps. These are the account's limits from step 2, shared by every campaign it sends for.
    3. Click Continue.
    4. Right before launch, re-open the sender list and confirm the names.

    HeyReach advises running no more than two campaigns at once from one account.

  8. 8
    Set the Schedule

    The schedule decides which days and hours HeyReach may act.

    1. On the Schedule tab, set the Start date. Add an End date if the campaign should stop on its own.
    2. Choose the Timezone, normally the one your buyers work in.
    3. Set the active hours with From and To.
    4. Tick the Days the campaign may run.
    5. Click Continue.
  9. 9
    Start the Campaign

    HeyReach creates campaigns as drafts. Nothing sends until you press Launch campaign.

    1. Read every message in the builder against your approved copy.
    2. Open every link in a real browser and check the tag is intact.
    3. On the Preview tab, check the lead list, schedule and senders, then press Launch campaign.
  10. 10
    Publish an Event on Your Company Page

    An event gives LinkedIn a second, free path to your registration page. The event drives clicks; the registration itself happens on your page.

    1. On a computer, open your company page as a super admin or content admin. Open Admin view → Create → Create an event, then complete the event form.
    2. Match the name, date, time and time zone to your event platform exactly. The organizer, event type and event format can't be changed after the event is created.
    3. Upload the banner and set the external registration link to your page with ?utm_source=linkedin on the end.
    4. Click Next, write the post text and click Post.
    5. Open the event and click Share, then Invite. Tick the connections and click Invite. Do this in daily batches.

    You can only invite your own 1st-degree connections, and LinkedIn allows each page admin 1,000 invitations a week across all events.

  11. 11
    Count LinkedIn Registrations and Check Daily

    An event with an external registration link gives you no attendee emails. People register on your own page, so your registration data is the only list of LinkedIn sign-ups, and the daily numbers show a problem before LinkedIn does.

    1. Count LinkedIn registrations by filtering your registration sheet or CRM to utm_source=linkedin. They already get the join link from your registration page, so there's nothing to import.
    2. Don't report the event's attendee count on LinkedIn as registrations. Only the people on your own list registered.
    3. Each day, open Campaigns, open the campaign and read Leads analytics: contacted, accepted and Failed leads. Steps analytics shows the reason each lead failed.
    4. Anyone who asks to stop goes on your do-not-contact list and into HeyReach under Settings → Blacklist. The Exclude leads on your blacklist box you ticked in step 4 keeps them out of that campaign.
