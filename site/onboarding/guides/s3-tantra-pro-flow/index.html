S3 · Multi-Channel Nurture

The Tantra Pro Flow

Six timed touches before the session, then an attended or no-show branch, across four channels.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Get registrants to actually turn up, then follow up with each person based on what they did. You'll send six timed touches across email, WhatsApp, SMS and an AI voice call, then split everyone into an attended branch and a no-show branch, and finally hand them all to the 52-week cadence. A scheduler and a set of behaviour tags run it without anyone pressing send. You're done when a test contact who attends and a test contact who deliberately misses both receive every touch on time and get the right tag automatically.

How This Changes by Mechanism

Purely time-triggered off one date: confirmation, T−7 days, T−24 hours at 8am their time, T−1 hour, T−15 minutes, then T−0 with SMS and the AI voice call added. Change the date in the spec and every touch moves together. Afterwards, attended people get the +0 offer and booking link, and no-shows get the replay after 24 hours with a real expiry.

3Step by Step
  1. 1
    Name Your Tags Once

    The Centre Does This

    The centre creates the tags in your CRM, and the funnel applies them: registered becomes webinar registrant, attended webinar attended, no-show live webinar not watched, replay watched watched webinar replay, and a payment purchased.

    Building it by hand instead

    Everything in this flow keys off tags on the contact. Name them once and never rename them:

    • webinar registrant: set by your page in Stage 2.
    • webinar attended and live webinar not watched: set from your event platform after the session.
    • watched webinar replay: set when someone watches the replay.
    • live webinar watched and webinar unsubscribed: also set by the platform sync, for a live viewer and for an opt-out.
    • purchased: set in Stage 5.

    Create each tag before you build on it. In GoHighLevel, open a workflow's Contact Tag trigger, click + Add New Tag and type the name exactly as above.

    A typo in a tag silently breaks every automation that listens for it.

  2. 2
    Collect Channel Opt-Ins on the Form

    The Centre Does This

    The form saves each person's consents with the contact, and the funnel only sends WhatsApp and SMS to people who opted in.

    Building it by hand instead

    Only message people on channels they agreed to. Add a tick box for WhatsApp and one for texts and calls on the registration form. Your Stage 2 backend turns each ticked box into a tag on the contact (for example whatsapp_reminder, the tag the reminder automation checks). A box left unticked means no tag, and no message on that channel.

    The scheduler only messages contacts with the matching opt-in tag.

  3. 3
    Turn on the Event Platform's Own Emails

    The Centre Does This

    The funnel's own scheduler sends every reminder in this flow. WebinarGeek still sends its own confirmation with each person's join link.

    Building it by hand instead

    Your event platform sends the confirmation and reminders natively, but the reminders are not there by default. A new WebinarGeek webinar has only the confirmation email and a draft replay email. Create the rest:

    1. Open the webinar and click Edit webinar → Emails.
    2. Click New email → Reminder email.
    3. Under Schedule email, set when it sends, then click Plan new email.
    4. Repeat for each send time, one reminder email each: the 1-day, the 1-hour and the live email.
    5. In each email, scroll to Send test e-mail and send yourself a copy. Do the same for the confirmation.

    If you move the broadcast, these send times don't move with it. Open each email, click Cancel planned emails and reschedule them.

  4. 4
    Write Every Touch With One Link

    Generate every message from your conversion engine, then set the mechanics by hand:

    • T−24 hours: 180 to 220 words. What to prepare before joining.
    • T−1 hour: 80 to 100 words. The join link, again.
    • We're live: 50 to 70 words, one link.

    Every message has exactly one link. Two links is a decision, and a decision at T−15 minutes is a lost attendee. Run every draft through a spam checker before it goes near a real list.

    Write every WhatsApp reminder as a plain statement of fact: the session starts tomorrow at 11:00, here is your link. Urgency words turn a utility template into a marketing one, which is charged at a higher rate and needs documented opt-in.

  5. 5
    Send the T−24 Hour Reminder at 8am Their Time

    The Centre Does This

    The funnel's scheduler runs every 5 minutes and sends the T−24 hour reminder at 08:00 in each recipient's own time zone, by email and WhatsApp.

    Building it by hand instead

    Not 8am in yours. A reminder that lands at 3am gets buried under everything that arrives after it.

    Store each registrant's time zone and send by it. If your tool can't send by the recipient's time zone, that's a reason to change tools. This one setting moves show-up more than most copy changes.

    In GoHighLevel:

    1. Open the workflow, then Settings, and set the time zone to Contact Timezone.
    2. In the Wait step, turn on Advance Window and set Resume Between Hours to start at 8:00.
    3. Have your backend save each registrant's time zone on the contact. A contact with none falls back to the account's time zone.

  6. 6
    Connect WhatsApp and Get the Templates Approved

    The Centre Does This

    The wa-templates job submits every approved WhatsApp touch to Meta as a template, points STOP replies at the funnel, checks the review for up to 48 hours and redeploys when they are approved.

    You Do This

    Register your WhatsApp sender in Twilio on its card and paste the number. Meta's business verification is yours to complete.

    Building it by hand instead

    Business WhatsApp is not the WhatsApp app: it is the WhatsApp Business Platform, reached through GoHighLevel or Twilio. Start it in week one, because Meta's business verification takes days.

    Connect the number in GoHighLevel:

    1. Get a phone number that has never been on WhatsApp, not the consumer app and not the Business app. Setting it up here ends WhatsApp on that number and the old chats do not transfer.
    2. In Meta Business Suite open Business Settings → Security Centre → Start Verification. Use the legal business name exactly as it appears on your documents, and upload the certificate of incorporation plus a utility bill or bank statement.
    3. In your GoHighLevel sub-account open Settings → WhatsApp → Connect. Log in with the business's Facebook account, choose the business, enter a plain display name (your business name, no emoji, no slogans), enter the number and type the code you receive.
    4. The number shows Connected. Send yourself a test from Conversations.

    Get the templates approved:

    1. Any WhatsApp message you start, outside the 24-hour window a registrant opens by messaging you first, must be an approved template. Every reminder is one.
    2. Open Settings → WhatsApp → Templates and create one template per reminder, with the join link as a variable. Write each as a plain statement of fact so Meta classes it as utility: urgency words make it marketing, which costs more and needs documented opt-in.
    3. Submit them for approval the same day you connect the number. Approval usually takes minutes to hours; a rejected template has to be rewritten and resubmitted. Templates are approved per sub-account, so a new account means submitting again.

    If you use Twilio instead:

    1. Create a Twilio account and upgrade it from the trial.
    2. Open Messaging → Senders → WhatsApp senders and click Create new sender. Pick a number that is not already on WhatsApp.
    3. Click Continue with Facebook. Choose or create your Meta Business Portfolio (you need admin access) and your WhatsApp Business Account, set the display name, and verify the number with the code.
    4. Complete Meta business verification to raise your sending limits. If Meta rejects the display name, you are capped at 250 business-started messages in 24 hours.
    5. Open Messaging → Content Template Builder and click Create new. Create a template for each reminder (T−24h, T−1h, T−15m, live) with the join link as a variable.
    6. Click Save and submit for WhatsApp approval and enter a sample value for each variable. Most approvals take minutes, but allow up to 48 hours.

    A button link takes only one variable, at the end of the address. Use a fixed address plus a variable ending, or put the full link in the message body.

    Keep SMS to the last hour: T−1h and T−15m, one link each, and only to people who opted in to texts. If you send SMS from GoHighLevel, set it up under Settings → Phone System → Trust Center. Before the first send:

    • Buy a Twilio number that can text your registrants' countries.
    • United States: register a brand and a campaign for A2P 10DLC ↗. It is required for any 10-digit number and takes up to two weeks.
    • India: local sending needs DLT registration with the mobile operators, and shortened links are not allowed. See Twilio's India guidelines ↗.
    • Any other country: check Twilio's SMS guidelines for it.
  7. 7
    Time the Reminders From the Broadcast Date

    The Centre Does This

    The scheduler sends the reminders from the event time: T−7 days (email), T−24 hours (email and WhatsApp), T−1 hour (email and WhatsApp), then WhatsApp at T−15 minutes and when you go live. SMS mirrors each WhatsApp touch once SMS is verified and the person opted in.

    Building it by hand instead

    Reminders are timed from the webinar_broadcast_date field that Stage 2 wrote on the contact, never from the moment someone registered.

    Start from the supplied GoHighLevel snapshot, which already contains this workflow, the confirmation workflow and the four custom fields: Import the snapshot ↗. Open it while signed in to GoHighLevel and choose the sub-account to import into; signed out, it shows a generic product page.

    In GoHighLevel, one workflow:

    1. Trigger: the registration, either the tag webinar registrant or the form submission from your page.
    2. Before each send, a Wait step set to wait until the field webinar_broadcast_date, with a negative offset for that reminder. Never use a plain wait for a number of days: it is wrong for everyone who did not register exactly that far ahead.
    3. Before each send, a check that the join link field is not empty, so no reminder goes out with a blank link. A contact whose date is already past skips that step instead of getting a burst of late reminders.
    4. Set the sub-account timezone (Settings → Business Profile) to your webinar's timezone, and give the workflow an exit on STOP or unsubscribe.

    The supplied snapshot ships this workflow with four WhatsApp sends: 3 days before at 09:00, 1 day before at 09:00, 4 hours 30 minutes before and 15 minutes before. Change the offsets to the schedule in step 4 of this guide, and turn the 4h30m one into a fixed morning time if your session starts early.

    To test without waiting days, move a test contact's webinar_broadcast_date to 65 minutes from now and watch the short reminders fire. Never edit the workflow's offsets to test.

    No GoHighLevel? Claude Code builds a scheduler from the prompt below that checks every 5 minutes and tags contacts whose session is within a reminder window. Clocks don't send webhooks, so on each run the scheduler does this:

    1. Find the next session and the minutes left until it starts.
    2. If it's within ±3 minutes of a reminder window (24h, 1h, 15m, live, then +1, +2 and +3 days), pick the contacts for that session who opted in.
    3. Skip anyone whose session ID doesn't match, so nobody gets another event's reminder.
    4. Tag them for that reminder; the tag triggers the send.
    5. Wait between batches, or messaging apps flag you.

    Pick where it runs before you build. On Vercel, a 5-minute schedule needs the Pro plan. The free Hobby plan runs a scheduled job once a day at most, and the deploy fails if you ask for more. Any other scheduler that can call your backend every 5 minutes works too. The job uses the same WebinarGeek key and GoHighLevel token as your Stage 2 backend.

  8. 8
    Add the AI Voice Call at Go-Live

    At T−0, an AI voice call reaches the people who still haven't joined. Keep it under 30 seconds: who's calling, that the session is starting now, and that the link is in their WhatsApp and email.

    Call only people who opted in, say plainly that it's an automated call, and stop calling anyone who says no. Automated calls are regulated: in the United States, that means the TCPA and state rules.

    ElevenLabs supplies the voice, and a Twilio number places the call:

    1. In ElevenLabs Agents, create a new agent and pick Blank template. Give it a first message, the 30-second script as its system prompt, and a voice. Test it.
    2. Open Phone Numbers and import your Twilio number. Fill in Label, Phone number, Twilio SID and Twilio Token. The number must be one you bought in Twilio, or a caller ID you verified there.
    3. Next to the number, click Outbound call. Select the agent, enter your own phone number and click Send Test Call.
    4. For the real run, have your backend start each call with ElevenLabs' outbound call API (POST /v1/convai/twilio/outbound-call), or upload the list as a batch call scheduled for the start time.
  9. 9
    Turn What They Did Into Tags

    The Centre Does This

    The attendance webhook from WebinarGeek tags each person attended, replay watched or no-show, with no CSV step. If no feed arrives, the funnel falls back to no-show three hours after the end. Zoom has no attendance feed, so Zoom events have no attended or no-show branches.

    Building it by hand instead

    After the session, your event platform reports who joined live, who watched the replay and who didn't show. An automation listens for each event, finds the contact in your CRM and applies the matching tag.

    WebinarGeek sends the live-viewer and replay-viewer events 1 to 2 hours after the session, not at the end. Start the attended and no-show branches from the tag the sync adds, or wait at least 2 hours after the scheduled end. A branch that runs at the end time reads attendance that hasn't arrived and sends every attendee 'sorry we missed you'.

    Connect the events:

    1. In WebinarGeek, open Account → Integrations → Webhooks. Paste your backend's address (it must start with https) into the URL field and click Connect. Add a secret so your backend can check each request is real.
    2. Click Add trigger and add New live viewer, New replay viewer and No show.
    3. Your backend finds the contact by email and applies webinar attended, watched webinar replay or live webinar not watched.

    Don't trust tags alone for who attended. Check viewing time too: 'did not watch' in the data doesn't always mean 'did not attend'. Filtering on the wrong flag has sent 'sorry you missed it' to people who watched the whole thing. Each subscriber in the WebinarGeek API has watched_live and watch_duration (in seconds), and under 30 seconds of viewing is not counted. The same numbers are under Statistics → Viewers.

  10. 10
    Build the Attended Branch

    The Centre Does This

    Everyone tagged attended gets the follow-ups at the end of the session and at +1, +2 and +3 days, without anyone starting them.

    Building it by hand instead

    They've seen the offer. Four touches:

    1. +0: the offer and the booking link, sent while they're still in the room.
    2. +1 day: a recap and the first objection from your buyer profile, answered.
    3. +2 days: proof and the second objection: a named result, not an adjective.
    4. +3 days: last call, with a real deadline. If there's no deadline, don't use the words.

    Build it in GoHighLevel:

    1. Open Automation → Workflows and click Create Workflow.
    2. Click Add New Trigger, choose Contact Tag, set the filter to Tag Added and pick webinar attended.
    3. Add the four messages, with a Wait step (a set period of time) between them.
    4. Switch Draft to Publish and click Save.
  11. 11
    Build the No-Show Branch

    The Centre Does This

    Everyone tagged no-show gets the replay follow-ups at +24 hours, +2 days and +5 days, without anyone starting them.

    Building it by hand instead

    They registered, so they're not cold. Four touches:

    1. +24 hours: the replay, with an expiry. No expiry means no reason to watch today.
    2. +2 days: the one thing they missed: one idea, with its timestamp, not 'here's the recording'.
    3. +5 days: the replay closes tonight. Then actually close it.
    4. Still nothing: the 30-day nurture. Unqualified is not the same as uninterested.

    Build the branch the same way as step 10, with live webinar not watched as the trigger tag.

    To close the replay for real, set its end date in WebinarGeek: Edit webinar → General → Dates and times → Replay → Settings, turn on Availability and set the Until date. The replay email is a draft until you schedule it: open it under Emails and use Schedule email.

  12. 12
    Attach Each Email Template by Hand

    In GoHighLevel, build each email under Marketing → Emails → Templates, inside a folder. Templates in a folder don't show at the top level, so open the folder to find them.

    Then open Automation → Workflows, open the workflow, click the email action and pick the template. This step can't be done through the API. It's always manual.

    Under the picked template is a Sync Edits to Template tick box. It is off on a new action, so later edits to the template do not reach this email. Tick it to keep the two the same.

Build the Reminder Scheduler
Build a scheduled job that runs every 5 minutes. Each run: fetch my next upcoming session from my event platform (broadcast ID and start time); compute minutes until start; if it is within ±3 minutes of one of these windows: 1440, 60, 15, 0 minutes before, or 1, 2, 3 days after, find CRM contacts whose broadcast_id matches and who carry the opt-in tag for the channel; add the window's tag (1_day_reminder, 1_hour_reminder, 15_min_reminder, going_live_reminder, followup_day_1/2/3) so my CRM workflow sends the message; format the date and time in each contact's own time zone; never tag the same contact twice for the same window; pause between batches.
