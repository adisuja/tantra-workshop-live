Paid Ads

Track the registration first, then test two ads on a small budget and scale only what converts.

1The Full Setup
2The Task

Buy traffic only after you can see what it buys. Track the registration as the conversion in Google Analytics and Meta (and Google Ads, if you run it), then test two ads that differ only in angle and judge them weekly by cost per registration. You're done when a test registration shows up in Google Analytics and Meta, and both ads are live with tagged links.

3Step by Step
  1. 1
    Make the Registration Your Conversion

    A conversion is the action you pay to get: the registration form submitted, not a page view. A new Google Analytics property has no registration event, so you create one. GA4 must already be on these pages (Stage 2 guide, step 7), including the thank-you page.

    1. In Google Analytics, open Admin → Data display → Events and click Create event.
    2. In Event name, type generate_lead and turn on Mark as key event.
    3. Choose Create without code, pick page_view in the Event name dropdown and enter your thank-you page address in URL (for example https://yoursite.com/thank-you).
    4. Click Create.
    5. Submit a test registration and confirm generate_lead appears in Reports → Realtime. Standard reports can take up to 24 hours.

  2. 2
    Install the Meta Pixel

    The Centre Does This

    Connect Meta on its card and the agent puts your pixel on every page of your funnel, and sends each registration to Meta from your funnel's server too (Conversions API). It checks the pixel is live before it builds anything else.

    Building it by hand instead

    The pixel tells Meta when someone visits or registers, so it can find more people like them. Meta now calls a pixel a dataset.

    You need a Meta business portfolio with an ad account, and a way to add code to your site.

    1. In Meta Events Manager, click Connect data → Web, name the dataset and click Create.
    2. Add the base code it gives you inside the <head> of every page, or use the partner integration for your site builder.
    3. Map the registration to the Lead event, so it fires when the thank-you page loads.
    4. Open Test events, enter your page address, load the page and check a PageView shows up.
  3. 3
    Add the Google Ads Conversion (Optional)

    Skip this step if you only run Meta. This guide launches a Meta campaign only, so this conversion is needed only if Google Ads will run. Google bids for what you mark as primary, so mark the registration.

    1. In Google Ads, open Goals, then Summary, and click Create conversion action.
    2. Choose Conversions on a website, enter your website domain and click Scan.
    3. If the scan finds no Google tag, add it to your site, or connect it through Google Tag Manager.
    4. Set the registration as a primary conversion.
  4. 4
    Start the Retargeting Pool on Day One

    The Centre Does This

    When you approve your ads, the agent builds the pool (visitors of the last 30 days who didn't register), a registrants audience and, once 100 people have registered, a 1% lookalike.

    Building it by hand instead

    A retargeting pool is everyone who visited your page but didn't register. It takes time to fill, so create it before any ad runs.

    1. In Meta, open Audiences → Create audience → Custom audience → Website.
    2. Pick your pixel (dataset) and choose all website visitors.
    3. Exclude people who fired the Lead event, so registrants drop out of the pool.
    4. Once the pool holds at least 100 people, build a lookalike audience from it.
  5. 5
    Write Two Ads That Differ Only in Angle

    If only the angle differs, the result tells you which message sells.

    1. Generate both from your Day 2 prompt pack: Ad A, benefit-led (the outcome your buyer wants) and Ad B, problem-led (their objection, in their words).
    2. Keep the same image style, page and audience.
    3. Tag each link utm_source=meta (or google), utm_medium=paid, utm_campaign={event_slug} and utm_content=variant_a or variant_b.

    Example, for a SaaS company: A "Plan every route before 7am", B "Still rebuilding the route plan by phone at 9am?"

  6. 6
    Launch on a Small Fixed Budget

    The Centre Does This

    The agent makes the ad images with Kie.ai and builds the campaign, ad set and both ads in your Meta account with your budget and countries, all paused. Switching the campaign on in Ads Manager stays with you. Leads from Meta instant forms or LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms register on your funnel too, if you pick them.

    Building it by hand instead

    This step spends money as soon as the campaign is active, so keep the first test small. You need a Facebook Page and a payment method on the ad account, or the campaign can't be published.

    1. In Meta Ads Manager, click Create and choose the Leads objective.
    2. In the ad set, set the conversion location to Website, pick your dataset and the Lead event, and add one audience.
    3. Set a small daily budget you can afford to learn with, for example $10 a day.
    4. Add the two ads and publish. Meta reviews every ad first, usually within 24 hours, so the ads aren't live at once.
    5. Change one variable per test, never budget and creative in the same week.
  7. 7
    Test Before You Trust It

    A broken page on a phone wastes every click you pay for.

    1. In Chrome, press F12, click Toggle device toolbar and check your pages as iPhone SE, iPad and a laptop.
    2. Do a real test registration yourself.
    3. Confirm it shows in Meta Events Manager → Test events and in Google Analytics Realtime.
    4. If you run Google Ads, check the Google tag fires in Tag Assistant ↗ and the conversion action's status in Goals, under Summary. Google only counts a conversion after an ad click, so none appears there until a campaign runs.
    5. Leave the ads alone for several days before reading results.
  8. 8
    Judge Each Ad Weekly by Cost per Registration

    The Centre Does This

    Your dashboard shows the last 7 days of spend, registrations and cost per registration from Meta, synced every hour.

    Building it by hand instead

    Clicks don't buy. Cost per registration against your target is the number that matters.

    Your target: the most you'd spend to win one buyer × show rate × close rate. For example, $300 per buyer × 40% show × 10% close = $12 per registration. Use your own numbers; these are only an illustration.

    1. Scale: at or under target. Add budget, not new variables.
    2. Pause: well over target, or no registrations after spending 3 times the target. Pause that ad.
    3. Hold and tune: in between. Change exactly one thing.
  9. 9
    Move the Money to the Channel That Buys

    Cost per buyer (spend on a channel divided by the buyers it brought) is the only fair comparison across channels.

    1. Once a month, rank every channel by cost per buyer. For registrations per channel, open Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition in Google Analytics, change the table's first column to Session source / medium and read the Key events column. GA4 must already be on these pages (Stage 2 guide, step 7).
    2. Move budget from the most expensive channel towards the cheapest, keeping total spend the same.
    3. Leave a small test budget on every channel, so you see when one gets cheap again.
