Day 1Strategize · Offer, Buyer, List and Channels

Decide What You Sell, and Exactly Who You Sell It To

Eight tasks, in order. Each adds a section to one strategy doc, and Day 2 builds every asset from it. Open a blank doc now (Google Docs, Word or Notion) and write every step there, not on this page. The Day 1 questions are separate: they produce your prompt pack.

Step 1: Write the Offer

Why: every Day 2 page, email and invite repeats this sentence. If a stranger cannot repeat it back, no channel will fix it.

  1. Fill the six slots, then join them into one sentence: I help who go from before to after using mechanism, in time, or guarantee.
  2. Read it aloud to someone outside your business. If they ask a follow-up question, rewrite it.
  3. Place it on your ladder (entry, core or flagship). Name and price the rung above it, built or not.
  4. Under the sentence, list the price, the guarantee, what is included and what is not.

The example, joined up: "We help B2B software companies of 11 to 50 staff go from ad hoc demos to a steady weekly demo calendar using a three-channel invite system, in 60 days, or we keep working at no charge until you get there."

Step 2: Position It With a Named Method and Voice

Why: an unnamed method sounds like every competitor. A named one gives buyers a reason to choose you, and gives Day 2 one name and one voice for every asset.

  1. Name your method in two to four words. List its three to five steps, with one line on why each step is needed.
  2. Write one line on why the obvious alternative (an agency, a tool alone, doing nothing) fails.
  3. Write your voice: three words you sound like, three you never sound like, and the words you always use. Add your brand colours, font and logo file.
  4. List your proof: each result with a number, a name and a date. None yet? Use borrowed proof (third-party research, a public case study, your own before and after) and label it as borrowed. Never invent one.

Example, for a SaaS company: "The Demo Engine: list, invite, remind, follow up. Ads alone fail because nobody named is invited; here every invite goes to a named buyer."

ToolYour strategy docTime20 minProofThe method has named steps and a reason for each

Step 3: Score the Offer on the Value Equation

Why: value is dream outcome times perceived likelihood, divided by time delay times effort. No headline saves a weak offer, so fix the weak lever before writing copy.

Pressure-test the value, then stack the offer
Top half: score the offer. Bottom half: the sentence you wrote in Step 1, rebuilt with the changes.
  1. Score each of the four levers out of 10, honestly.
  2. Write one change next to each: a number and deadline for the outcome, proof a sceptic sees in ten seconds, a week-one win, one step you do for the buyer.
  3. Make the changes to your two lowest scores, then update your Step 1 sentence.

Example, for a SaaS company: time delay scores 3 out of 10 because setup takes six weeks. The change: "first campaign live in week one, and we do the setup."

ToolYour strategy docTime30 minProofEvery lever has a written change, and the two lowest are made

Step 4: Define the Buyer Persona

Why: the account (fields 1 to 4) plus the role (field 5) is what you type into a data vendor in Step 5. The person (fields 5 to 9) is who your copy speaks to. The trigger (field 10) says when to reach them.

From a whole market to one winnable account, then to one person
Both halves or neither. The account tells you where to find them; the person tells you what to say.

  1. List your 20 best customers (by revenue, retention and ease to serve) in a sheet. Fewer? Use them all. None yet? Use your 10 best sales conversations and mark each value "assumed".
  2. Fill fields 1 to 4 for each. Any value shared by more than half of them goes into the persona.
  3. Fill fields 5 to 10 from real conversations. Quote the objection that kills your deals word for word.
  4. Write down what your worst five customers share. Those are your exclusions. Write at least three.
  5. No recent conversations? Tonight, ask five customers: "What number are you personally judged on this quarter, and what's in the way?"

Example, for a SaaS company: 11 to 50 staff, B2B software, US and UK, head of sales, judged on pipeline created. The objection: "We tried outbound and it burned our domain."

  1. Size the list first, working backwards: monthly revenue target divided by offer price is the buyers you need. Divide by each rate you can evidence (purchase, show-up, registration) to get the number of people to invite. No evidence for a rate? Write "unknown, will measure"; never borrow a benchmark. If the number is absurd, change the price, offer or funnel now.
  2. Pick one database. Enter persona fields 1 to 5 as filters and your exclusions as negative filters.
  3. Export a first list of a few hundred to 1,000 people into a sheet: first name, last name, title, company, website, email, LinkedIn URL, country.
  4. Remove duplicates by email, your existing customers, and anyone who matches an exclusion.
  5. Verify every email and keep only the valid ones.
  6. Check the rules on cold outreach in your buyers' countries (for example GDPR in the EU and UK) before anything sends.

Example, for a SaaS company: in Apollo, titles "Head of Sales" and "VP Sales", 11 to 50 employees, Computer Software, United States. Export, remove customers, verify, save as the Day 2 invite list.

Step 6: Pick Your Funnel

Why: the funnel decides which Day 2 guide you build and where your hours go. Pick it on what your buyers already believe and who closes the sale, not on the product.

Twenty funnel types, grouped by the job they do
◆ the three shapes we build in depth (reverse squeeze has two variants)hours = our one-time build estimate, not weekly load
The type decides which stage carries the weight, and so where your build hours go.
  1. Answer the five questions in Your Conversion Mechanism at the end of this chapter (belief, ladder rung, who closes, weekly hours, your leaning). It recommends a funnel.
  2. Check it against belief, the answer that decides most picks. Buyers who know you and need one idea reframed: a webinar. Buyers who must try it to believe it: a workshop or challenge. Buyers who have never heard of you: a reverse squeeze.
  3. Write one sentence naming your funnel and the answer that decided it. Give the same answer to "Which funnel are you building?" in today's questions, so Day 2 opens on your guide.
  4. Book the build hours in your calendar. The diagram's hours are one-time build estimates. Running it takes weekly time on top (our estimates): 8 to 12 h for a webinar, 10 to 15 h during a workshop cohort, 4 to 8 h for a reverse squeeze.

Example, for a SaaS company: buyers know the category but not the method, and a person closes on a demo call. It picks the webinar: "They need one idea reframed, and a person closes."

Step 7: Set the Webinar Topic, Date and Time

Why: every Day 2 invite, reminder and page carries the topic and date. Set them once, so nothing is rewritten.

  1. Topic: the buyer's outcome plus your method's name. Would your persona give up an hour for it?
  2. Date: new sending domains need 2 to 3 weeks of warm-up before cold email (Step 8), and invites need time to land after that. Count forward from today.
  3. Time: in your audience's time zone, not yours; across zones, the hour most of them are at work.
  4. Length and replay: session length, and how long the recording stays up.
  5. Write it as one line: title, date, time with time zone, length, platform.

Reverse squeeze: set the day the video goes live. Challenge: set the date of day one.

Example, for a SaaS company: "How B2B Software Teams Fill Their Demo Calendar Without Ads. the Thursday after your workshop, 11:00 ET (16:00 UK), 60 minutes, on Zoom."

ToolYour calendarTime15 minProofOne line with title, date, time zone, length and platform

Step 8: Connect Every Channel and the Cloudflare API

Why: domain warm-up is the only task with a fixed wait, so it starts today. The Cloudflare token lets your AI agent add DNS records and deploy the Day 3 automations for you.

  1. Buy domains: two or three outreach domains that resemble your main one, from any registrar. Never send cold email from your main domain. Later, redirect each one to your main website (a Redirect Rule in Cloudflare), so anyone who types it lands somewhere real.
  2. Move them to Cloudflare: add each as a site, then switch its nameservers at your registrar to the two Cloudflare shows. Do this first: the DNS token in item 11 can only cover domains already in Cloudflare.
  3. Add them to Google Workspace (a paid plan; every mailbox is a paid seat): in the Admin console, Account → Domains → Manage domains → Add a domain, as a secondary domain. Add the verification TXT record it shows in Cloudflare under DNS → Records, then click Verify. (Microsoft 365: Settings → Domains → Add domain.)
  4. Add the MX record in Cloudflare so replies arrive: type MX, name @, mail server SMTP.GOOGLE.COM, priority 1. (Microsoft 365 shows its own MX value.)
  5. Create mailboxes: a few per domain.
  6. Authenticate: in the Admin console, Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email, pick the domain, then Generate new record with the default selector google. Add the DKIM, SPF and DMARC records in Cloudflare, then click Start authentication. (Microsoft 365: the Defender portal's email authentication settings, DKIM tab.)
  7. Warm-up: connect every mailbox to Instantly (the Day 2 Cold Email guide's tool) or your own sending tool, and switch warm-up on.
  8. LinkedIn: connect your account to your LinkedIn outreach tool (the Day 2 guide uses HeyReach). Make your headline state the Step 1 outcome.
  9. WhatsApp: register a number on the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API, not the WhatsApp Business app) through your sending tool or a WhatsApp provider. Then submit your reminder templates today: approval can take days, and reminders go only to people who opted in.
  10. Calendar: connect the calendar you take calls on (Google Calendar or Outlook) and create a discovery-call booking link.
  11. Cloudflare API: My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token, template Edit zone DNS, your outreach domains under Zone Resources. Running Day 3's automations on Cloudflare Workers? Create a second token from Edit Cloudflare Workers.
  12. Store each token in your password manager or your project's .env file, never in a doc or chat. Test it: curl https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/user/tokens/verify -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_TOKEN" should answer "This API Token is valid and active".

Click-by-click screens: the Day 2 Cold Email guide, steps 1 to 6.

Example, for a SaaS company: it sells from northwind.io, buys northwindhq.com and trynorthwind.com, creates two mailboxes on each, and starts warm-up today for a webinar three weeks out.
