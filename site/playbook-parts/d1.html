Lock the Offer, the Buyer and the Channels Every Asset Is Built On

Every revenue system that compounds starts with decisions most companies never make on purpose: exactly what you sell, to whom, at what price and through which channels. Day 1 makes all of them. This chapter explains the eight steps in order: what each one is, why it matters and what good looks like. You carry them out one at a time in your onboarding centre (or through the Day 1 questions), and each adds a section to the strategy document Day 2 builds every asset from.

Step 1: Write the Offer

Why: every Day 2 page, email and invite repeats this sentence. If a stranger cannot repeat it back, no channel will fix it.

BeforeWhere they are stuck todayAd hoc demos

  1. Fill the six slots, then join them into one sentence: I help who go from before to after using mechanism, in time, or guarantee.
  2. Read it aloud to someone outside your business. If they ask a follow-up question, rewrite it.
  3. Place it on your ladder (entry, core or flagship). Name and price the rung above it, built or not.
  4. Under the sentence, list the price, the guarantee, what is included and what is not.

The same sentence for three kinds of business:

  • Software company: "We help B2B software companies of 11 to 50 staff go from ad hoc demos to a steady weekly demo calendar using a three-channel invite system, in 60 days, or we keep working at no charge until you get there."
  • Consultancy: "We help managing partners of accounting firms go from waiting on referrals to three new retainers a quarter using the Partner Pipeline System, in 90 days, or we keep working at no charge until it runs."
  • Course business: "We help heads of sales at companies of 50 to 300 staff go from a five-month ramp for every new manager to a nine-week ramp using The Manager Sprint, in one six-week cohort, or anyone who misses a week repeats it in the next cohort free."

Step 2: Position It With a Named Method and Voice

Why: an unnamed method sounds like every competitor. A named one gives buyers a reason to choose you, and gives Day 2 one name and one voice for every asset.

  1. Name your method in two to four words. List its three to five steps, with one line on why each step is needed.
  2. Write one line on why the obvious alternative (an agency, a tool alone, doing nothing) fails.
  3. Write your voice: three words you sound like, three you never sound like, and the words you always use. Add your brand colours, font and logo file.
  4. List your proof: each result with a number, a name and a date. None yet? Use borrowed proof (third-party research, a public case study, your own before and after) and label it as borrowed. Never invent one.

Example, for a consultancy: "The Partner Pipeline System: map the referral pattern you already have, turn it into one offer, build one repeatable channel, hand the rhythm to the team. Agencies fail because they sell activity, not a channel the firm owns."

ToolYour strategy docTime20 minProofThe method has named steps and a reason for each

Step 3: Score the Offer on the Value Equation

Why: value is dream outcome times perceived likelihood, divided by time delay times effort. This is Alex Hormozi's value equation from $100M Offers; Hormozi sold a majority stake in his portfolio of companies, including Gym Launch, and now runs the investment firm Acquisition.com. No headline saves a weak offer, so fix the weak lever before writing copy.

Pressure-test the value, then stack the offer
Top half: score the offer. Bottom half: the sentence you wrote in Step 1, rebuilt with the changes.
  1. Score each of the four levers out of 10, honestly.
  2. Write one change next to each: a number and deadline for the outcome, proof a sceptic sees in ten seconds, a week-one win, one step you do for the buyer.
  3. Make the changes to your two lowest scores, then update your Step 1 sentence.

Example, for a course business: perceived likelihood scores 4 out of 10 because buyers have paid for training their managers never finished. The change: "attendance tracked every week, and anyone who misses a week repeats it in the next cohort free."

Example, for a software company: time delay scores 3 out of 10 because setup takes six weeks. The change: "first campaign live in week one, and we do the setup."

ToolYour strategy docTime30 minProofEvery lever has a written change, and the two lowest are made

Step 4: Define the Buyer Persona

Why: the account (fields 1 to 4) plus the role (field 5) is what you type into a data vendor in Step 5. The person (fields 5 to 9) is who your copy speaks to. The trigger (field 10) says when to reach them.

From a whole market to one winnable account, then to one person
Both halves or neither. The account tells you where to find them; the person tells you what to say.

  1. List your 20 best customers (by revenue, retention and ease to serve) in a sheet. Fewer? Use them all. None yet? Use your 10 best sales conversations and mark each value "assumed".
  2. Fill fields 1 to 4 for each. Any value shared by more than half of them goes into the persona.
  3. Fill fields 5 to 10 from real conversations. Quote the objection that kills your deals word for word.
  4. Write down what your worst five customers share. Those are your exclusions. Write at least three.
  5. No recent conversations? Tonight, ask five customers: "What number are you personally judged on this quarter, and what's in the way?"

Example, for a software company: 11 to 50 staff, B2B software, US and UK, head of sales, judged on pipeline created. The objection: "We tried outbound and it burned our domain."

Example, for a consultancy: 25 to 120 staff, accounting and law firms, UK, managing partner, judged on new fees billed. The objection: "Our work comes from referrals. Marketing doesn't work for firms like ours."

Step 5: Build the Prospect List

Why: Day 2's invites go to named people with verified contact details. Unverified addresses bounce and damage the sending domains from Step 8.

Prices change often. Check each vendor's pricing page before you buy.
  1. Size the list first, working backwards: monthly revenue target divided by offer price is the buyers you need. Divide by each rate you can evidence (purchase, show-up, registration) to get the number of people to invite. No evidence for a rate? Write "unknown, will measure"; never borrow a benchmark. If the number is absurd, change the price, offer or funnel now.
  2. Pick one database. Enter persona fields 1 to 5 as filters and your exclusions as negative filters.
  3. Export a first list of a few hundred to 1,000 people into a sheet: first name, last name, title, company, website, email, LinkedIn URL, country.
  4. Remove duplicates by email, your existing customers, and anyone who matches an exclusion.
  5. Verify every email and keep only the valid ones.
  6. Check the rules on cold outreach in your buyers' countries (for example GDPR in the EU and UK) before anything sends.

Example, for a consultancy: in Apollo, titles "Managing Partner" and "Partner", 25 to 120 employees, Accounting, United Kingdom. Export, remove existing clients, verify, save as the Day 2 invite list.

Step 6: Pick Your Funnel

Why: the funnel decides which Day 2 guide you build and where your hours go. Pick it on what your buyers already believe and who closes the sale, not on what you sell. Every type in the diagram below is explained, with its own animated funnel, under The Big Picture.

Twenty funnel types, grouped by the job they do
◆ the three shapes we build in depth (reverse squeeze has two variants)hours = our one-time build estimate, not weekly load
The type decides which stage carries the weight, and so where your build hours go.
  1. Answer the five questions in Your Conversion Mechanism at the end of this chapter (belief, ladder rung, who closes, weekly hours, your leaning). It recommends a funnel.
  2. Check it against your kind of business. New to funnels, or still finding the message that lands: a webinar, which suits every B2B business. You already know which messaging works and have case studies to show: a reverse squeeze. Your customers must learn or do something before they feel the value (consulting with heavy knowledge transfer, training, edtech): a workshop series, also called a challenge.
  3. Then check it against belief, the answer that decides most picks. Buyers who know you and need one idea reframed: a webinar. Buyers who must try it to believe it: a workshop series. Buyers who have never heard of you: a reverse squeeze.
  4. Write one sentence naming your funnel and the answer that decided it. Give the same answer to "Which funnel are you building?" in today's questions, so Day 2 opens on your guide.
  5. Book the build hours in your calendar. The diagram's hours are one-time build estimates. Running it takes weekly time on top (our estimates): 8 to 12 h for a webinar, 10 to 15 h during a workshop cohort, 4 to 8 h for a reverse squeeze.

Example, for a software company: buyers know the category but not the method, and a person closes on a demo call. It picks the webinar: "They need one idea reframed, and a person closes."

Example, for a course business: managers must try the method before their boss buys a team pack. It picks the workshop series: "They need to feel it work before they pay."

Step 7: Set the Webinar Topic, Date and Time

Why: every Day 2 invite, reminder and page carries the topic and date. Set them once, so nothing is rewritten.

  1. Topic: the buyer's outcome plus your method's name. Would your persona give up an hour for it?
  2. Date: new sending domains need 2 to 3 weeks of warm-up before cold email (Step 8), and invites need time to land after that. Count forward from today.
  3. Time: in your audience's time zone, not yours; across zones, the hour most of them are at work.
  4. Length and replay: session length, and how long the recording stays up.
  5. Write it as one line: title, date, time with time zone, length, platform.

Reverse squeeze: set the day the video goes live. Challenge: set the date of day one.

Example, for a consultancy: "How Accounting Firms Win New Retainers Without Waiting for Referrals. A Thursday at least three weeks out, 08:00 London, 45 minutes, on Zoom."

ToolYour calendarTime15 minProofOne line with title, date, time zone, length and platform

Step 8: Connect Every Channel and the Cloudflare API

Why: domain warm-up is the only task with a fixed wait, so it starts today. The Cloudflare token lets your AI agent add DNS records and deploy the Day 3 automations for you.

  1. Get domains: two or three outreach domains that resemble your main one: ones you own at GoDaddy, or new ones registered at Cloudflare Registrar. Never send cold email from your main domain. Later, redirect each one to your main website (a Redirect Rule in Cloudflare), so anyone who types it lands somewhere real.
  2. DNS in Cloudflare: domains registered at Cloudflare Registrar are there already. For GoDaddy domains, add each as a site in Cloudflare, then switch its nameservers in GoDaddy to the two Cloudflare shows. Do this first: the DNS token in item 11 can only cover domains already in Cloudflare.
  3. Add them to Google Workspace (a paid plan; every mailbox is a paid seat): in the Admin console, Account → Domains → Manage domains → Add a domain, as a secondary domain. Add the verification TXT record it shows in Cloudflare under DNS → Records, then click Verify.
  4. Add the MX record in Cloudflare so replies arrive: type MX, name @, mail server SMTP.GOOGLE.COM, priority 1.
  5. Create mailboxes: a few per domain.
  6. Authenticate: in the Admin console, Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email, pick the domain, then Generate new record with the default selector google. Add the DKIM, SPF and DMARC records in Cloudflare, then click Start authentication.
  7. Warm-up: connect every mailbox in Tantra (Accounts, a Google sign-in per mailbox). Tantra warms each one from then on.
  8. LinkedIn: connect your account in Tantra (Channels), signing in once. Make your headline state the Step 1 outcome.
  9. WhatsApp: register your WhatsApp sender (a number that is not on the WhatsApp app) so Tantra can send your reminders. Submit the reminder templates today: approval can take days, and reminders go only to people who opted in.
  10. Calendar: connect the Google Calendar you take calls on to Cal.com and create a discovery-call booking link.
  11. Cloudflare API: My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token, template Edit zone DNS, your outreach domains under Zone Resources. Running Day 3's automations on Cloudflare Workers? Create a second token from Edit Cloudflare Workers.
  12. Store each token in your password manager or your project's .env file, never in a doc or chat. Test it: curl https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/user/tokens/verify -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_TOKEN" should answer "This API Token is valid and active".

Click-by-click screens: the Day 2 Cold Email guide, steps 1 to 6.

Example, for a software company: it sells from northwind.io, buys northwindhq.com and trynorthwind.com, creates two mailboxes on each, and starts warm-up today for a webinar three weeks out.

Which Plan

The free Cloudflare plan is enough. Domains you register are charged at Cloudflare's cost price, on the card in your Cloudflare account.

Permissions to Grant

  • Zone, DNS, Workers Scripts and D1, each at Edit (the link on your card ticks these for you).
  • Account, Registrar: Domains, Admin (only on the Cloudflare Registrar path; Cloudflare offers only Read or Admin here).
  • Zone, Single Redirect, Edit.
  • Account Resources: All accounts. Zone Resources: All zones, so domains you buy later are covered.

Where the Key Appears

After Create Token, Cloudflare shows the token once on the success page. Copy it there with the copy icon; it never appears again.

Common Errors

The card says a permission is missing
Edit the token in Cloudflare under My Profile, API Tokens and add the permission it names, then click Connect again.
No domain can be registered
Registrar: Domains must be at Admin. Read lets the agent look, not buy.
Your token reaches more than one account
Paste the Account ID from the right side of your Cloudflare overview page into the second field.

Your Day 1 Summary
  1. Your offer is one sentence a stranger can repeat, scored on the value equation and placed on a ladder.
  2. Your method, voice and proof are written once, so every asset sounds the same.
  3. Your buyer is defined as an account and a person, and a verified list of them exists.
  4. Your funnel, event date and every channel are set, with domain warm-up already running.
  5. It all lives in one strategy doc with five blocks (offer, buyer, voice, method and proof). Day 2 builds every asset from it.
