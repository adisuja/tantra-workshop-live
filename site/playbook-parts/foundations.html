FoundationsThe Thing You're Actually Building

What Is AI RevOps?

RevOps is one word for something most companies run as two departments, and the gap between those two departments is where most revenue quietly dies.

Marketing owns the top: ads, content, posts, emails and messages. Sales owns the bottom: the call, the demo, the proposal, the close. Marketing counts leads, sales counts deals, and nobody owns the distance between them.

Revenue operations is the decision to stop running it as two jobs. One system, one owner, one set of numbers, from the first time a stranger sees your name to the moment the money clears.

An agent reads the CRM, writes the sequence, watches the step rates, spots the drop, drafts the fix and schedules the send.

◆ So what does the "AI" in AI RevOps actually mean

Not a chatbot on a website. The operator of the system is an agent. You still decide the strategy, the offer and what ships. The agent does the reading, writing, wiring and measuring that used to be split across a marketer, an ops person and a sales manager.

FoundationsWhy This Shape, and Not Just a Better Product

Why Do You Need a Funnel (A Conversion Engine)?

People buy on a promise and stay on a result. Most funnels only address the first.

You can earn the yes and still lose the customer, because nothing you built covers the gap between paying and the product working for them. That gap is where churn lives, and the campaign dashboard never shows it.

So a conversion engine is not a machine for getting a yes. It's a machine for delivering value continuously: before the sale, to earn the yes, and after it, to make the yes hold.

Excitement peaks at purchase. Value arrives later. The gap is the churn window.

Why the Sale Alone Doesn't Hold It

Software is not sticky on its own. The switching cost comes from what the customer has built inside it, and they only build once someone has taught them. Russell Brunson calls this the pain of disconnect and credits it to Bill Glazer. So teach the first win fast: restate their goal, deliver one quick win, and book the next step before the first session ends. That recipe is Alex Hormozi's, and so is the principle behind it: giving value today is what makes people expect value tomorrow.

Then keep them. Dan Martell's retention list has four parts: an on-ramp that teaches the basics before first use, a peer community, visible case studies, and a member-at-risk monitor.

Four different mechanisms need four different assets

Why Every Brand You Can Name Already Does This

Well-known software and service brands publish content and run live sessions continuously, and most add a book, workshops and events, and a community. Each is a conversion engine: it moves someone cold one step closer to buying, then keeps them warm after they buy.

The same five assets, working both sides of the sale
You are not building five things. You're building five wrappers around one engine, and each one keeps earning after the sale, which is the half almost everybody skips.
◆ Results Alone Plateau

Helping someone achieve a result is not enough on its own, which is Brunson's point in Expert Secrets. Teaching gets customers to the result; community and a visible next rung keep them past it. Build retention too, or customers go quiet around month nine.

◆ What this means for the next three days

Every page, sequence and automation you build is in service of one thing: moving the value crossing earlier and then continuing to deliver value on the far side of the sale. If a thing you're about to build does neither, it's decoration. Cut it.

Where Your Offers Live: The Value Ladder

Before you pick a funnel type, you need to know what the funnel is selling into. Almost nobody buys the expensive thing first. They buy a small thing, it works, and they climb.

A funnel is not a machine for selling one product. It is a machine for moving people up a value ladder: each rung asks for more commitment and returns more value, and fewer people reach each rung.

The value ladder: commitment rises, audience narrows, value compounds
The load-bearing rung is the entry offer. Price it so the revenue it produces covers the cost of the traffic that produced it, and your top of funnel becomes free. Every rung above it is then pure margin.
◆ Example · this workshop's own ladder

You're standing on one right now. Free content brought you here. The 3-Day AI RevOps Workshop is the entry offer, deliberately priced to cover the cost of filling it. Inside it you get a month of the app included. Above it sits a core program (course, community, group coaching) and above that a flagship tier (long-horizon access plus one-to-one implementation). Four rungs, one system, and each rung sells the next by working.

◆ Tip · Build the Ladder Before the Funnel

Two rungs are much easier than one. With a single offer, the funnel has to do all the persuading in one shot. If you don't have a second rung yet, write one down now; you'll need it by Day 3.

◆ Why retention is the whole game

Acquiring a customer costs money; keeping one costs far less. So the north star of this system is not the first sale. It is recurring revenue that compounds month after month. Everything before it exists to earn the retention Stage 6 builds.

The One Equation to Memorize

The funnel equation

Profit = (Traffic × Conversion rate × Price) − Cost to deliver

Pull any one lever up and profit moves. A funnel lets you pull them on purpose instead of by accident.

Its flaw: "conversion rate" is a guess. On Day 3 you replace every symbol in that equation with a number your dashboard actually reports (a counted event with a named source), then read those numbers weekly and fix whichever one is lowest.

◆ The rule that runs through the whole week

Never model your funnel with invented rates. Every step you build gets instrumented as an event. The count of that event is the metric. A "conversion rate" is just one count divided by another. If you can't fetch it, you can't fix it, and you'll be optimising by vibes.
