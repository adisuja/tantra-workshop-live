FoundationsThe Thing You're Actually Building

What Is AI RevOps?

RevOps is one word for something most companies run as two departments, and the gap between those two departments is where most revenue quietly dies.

Marketing owns the top: ads, content, posts, emails and messages. Sales owns the bottom: the call, the demo, the proposal, the close. Marketing counts leads, sales counts deals, and nobody owns the distance between them.

Revenue operations is the decision to stop running it as two jobs. One system, one owner, one set of numbers, from the first time a stranger sees your name to the moment the money clears.

Cold stranger to cleared payment: one system, not two departments
Read the top row as a set of numbers, not a story. Seven stages, seven counts, six rates between them. The moment you own all of them you stop arguing about whose fault a bad quarter was.

The four layers in the lower half of the diagram (touchpoints, people, data and plumbing) have always existed. What is new is that software that can reason can now operate all four through their APIs. An agent reads the CRM, writes the sequence, watches the step rates, spots the drop, drafts the fix and schedules the send.

◆ So what does the "AI" in AI RevOps actually mean

Not a chatbot on a website. The operator of the system is an agent. You still decide the strategy, the offer and what ships. The agent does the reading, writing, wiring and measuring that used to be split across a marketer, an ops person and a sales manager.

◆ Where you'll be by Friday

You can stand this system up yourself, with an agent operating it: the prompts, templates, workflows and automations to run it from cold stranger to cleared payment, measured end to end.

FoundationsWhy This Shape, and Not Just a Better Product

Why Do You Need a Funnel (A Conversion Engine)?

People buy on a promise and stay on a result. Most funnels only address the first.

You can earn the yes and still lose the customer, because nothing you built covers the gap between paying and the product working for them. That gap is where churn lives, and the campaign dashboard never shows it.

So a conversion engine is not a machine for getting a yes. It's a machine for delivering value continuously: before the sale, to earn the yes, and after it, to make the yes hold.

Excitement peaks at purchase. Value arrives later. The gap is the churn window.

Why the Sale Alone Doesn't Hold It

Software is not sticky on its own. The switching cost comes from what the customer has built inside it, and they only build once someone has taught them. Russell Brunson calls this the pain of disconnect and credits it to Bill Glazer. So teach the first win fast: restate their goal, deliver one quick win, and book the next step before the first session ends. That recipe is Alex Hormozi's, and so is the principle behind it: giving value today is what makes people expect value tomorrow.

Then keep them. Dan Martell's retention list has four parts: an on-ramp that teaches the basics before first use, a peer community, visible case studies, and a member-at-risk monitor.

Four different mechanisms need four different assets

Why Every Brand You Can Name Already Does This

Well-known software and service brands publish content and run live sessions continuously, and most add a book, workshops and events, and a community. Each is a conversion engine: it moves someone cold one step closer to buying, then keeps them warm after they buy.

The same five assets, working both sides of the sale
You are not building five things. You're building five wrappers around one engine, and each one keeps earning after the sale, which is the half almost everybody skips.
◆ Results Alone Plateau

Helping someone achieve a result is not enough on its own, which is Brunson's point in Expert Secrets. Teaching gets customers to the result; community and a visible next rung keep them past it. Build retention too, or customers go quiet around month nine.

◆ What this means for the next three days

Every page, sequence and automation you build is in service of one thing: moving the value crossing earlier and then continuing to deliver value on the far side of the sale. If a thing you're about to build does neither, it's decoration. Cut it.

Every Funnel Has 4 Jobs

Traffic, capture, convert, retain. Every tool, page and automation you build this week does one of those four jobs. Most businesses are good at one and weak at the rest: a viral post with no capture step leaks every visitor, and a beautiful sales page with no traffic is a billboard in the desert. The funnel's job is to make all four work together, and to show you which one is broken.

Where Your Offers Live: The Value Ladder

Before you pick a funnel type, you need to know what the funnel is selling into. Almost nobody buys the expensive thing first. They buy a small thing, it works, and they climb.

A funnel is not a machine for selling one product. It is a machine for moving people up a value ladder: each rung asks for more commitment and returns more value, and fewer people reach each rung.

The value ladder: commitment rises, audience narrows, value compounds
The load-bearing rung is the entry offer. Price it so the revenue it produces covers the cost of the traffic that produced it, and your top of funnel becomes free. Every rung above it is then pure margin.
◆ Example · this workshop's own ladder

You're standing on one right now. Free content brought you here. The 3-Day AI RevOps Workshop is the entry offer, deliberately priced to cover the cost of filling it. Inside it you get a month of the app included. Above it sits a core program (course, community, group coaching) and above that a flagship tier (long-horizon access plus one-to-one implementation). Four rungs, one system, and each rung sells the next by working.

◆ Tip · Build the Ladder Before the Funnel

Two rungs are much easier than one. With a single offer, the funnel has to do all the persuading in one shot. If you don't have a second rung yet, write one down now; you'll need it by Day 3.

◆ Why retention is the whole game

Acquiring a customer costs money; keeping one costs far less. So the north star of this system is not the first sale. It is recurring revenue that compounds month after month. Everything before it exists to earn the retention Stage 6 builds.

The One Equation to Memorize

The funnel equation

Profit = (Traffic × Conversion rate × Price) − Cost to deliver

Pull any one lever up and profit moves. A funnel lets you pull them on purpose instead of by accident.

Its flaw: "conversion rate" is a guess. On Day 3 you replace every symbol in that equation with a number your dashboard actually reports (a counted event with a named source), then read those numbers weekly and fix whichever one is lowest.

◆ The rule that runs through the whole week

Never model your funnel with invented rates. Every step you build gets instrumented as an event. The count of that event is the metric. A "conversion rate" is just one count divided by another. If you can't fetch it, you can't fix it, and you'll be optimising by vibes.

Step 2: Position It With a Named Method and Voice

Why: an unnamed method sounds like every competitor. A named one gives buyers a reason to choose you, and gives Day 2 one name and one voice for every asset.

  1. Name your method in two to four words. List its three to five steps, with one line on why each step is needed.
  2. Write one line on why the obvious alternative (an agency, a tool alone, doing nothing) fails.
  3. Write your voice: three words you sound like, three you never sound like, and the words you always use. Add your brand colours, font and logo file.
  4. List your proof: each result with a number, a name and a date. None yet? Use borrowed proof (third-party research, a public case study, your own before and after) and label it as borrowed. Never invent one.

Example, for a SaaS company: "The Demo Engine: list, invite, remind, follow up. Ads alone fail because nobody named is invited; here every invite goes to a named buyer."

Step 3: Score the Offer on the Value Equation

Why: value is dream outcome times perceived likelihood, divided by time delay times effort. No headline saves a weak offer, so fix the weak lever before writing copy.

Pressure-test the value, then stack the offer
Top half: score the offer. Bottom half: the sentence you wrote in Step 1, rebuilt with the changes.
  1. Score each of the four levers out of 10, honestly.
  2. Write one change next to each: a number and deadline for the outcome, proof a sceptic sees in ten seconds, a week-one win, one step you do for the buyer.
  3. Make the changes to your two lowest scores, then update your Step 1 sentence.

Example, for a SaaS company: time delay scores 3 out of 10 because setup takes six weeks. The change: "first campaign live in week one, and we do the setup."

Step 8: Connect Every Channel and the Cloudflare API

Why: domain warm-up is the only task with a fixed wait, so it starts today. The Cloudflare token lets your AI agent add DNS records and deploy the Day 3 automations for you.

  1. Buy domains: two or three outreach domains that resemble your main one, from any registrar. Never send cold email from your main domain. Later, redirect each one to your main website (a Redirect Rule in Cloudflare), so anyone who types it lands somewhere real.
  2. Move them to Cloudflare: add each as a site, then switch its nameservers at your registrar to the two Cloudflare shows. Do this first: the DNS token in item 11 can only cover domains already in Cloudflare.
  3. Add them to Google Workspace (a paid plan; every mailbox is a paid seat): in the Admin console, Account → Domains → Manage domains → Add a domain, as a secondary domain. Add the verification TXT record it shows in Cloudflare under DNS → Records, then click Verify. (Microsoft 365: Settings → Domains → Add domain.)
  4. Add the MX record in Cloudflare so replies arrive: type MX, name @, mail server SMTP.GOOGLE.COM, priority 1. (Microsoft 365 shows its own MX value.)
  5. Create mailboxes: a few per domain.
  6. Authenticate: in the Admin console, Apps → Google Workspace → Gmail → Authenticate email, pick the domain, then Generate new record with the default selector google. Add the DKIM, SPF and DMARC records in Cloudflare, then click Start authentication. (Microsoft 365: the Defender portal's email authentication settings, DKIM tab.)
  7. Warm-up: connect every mailbox to Instantly (the Day 2 Cold Email guide's tool) or your own sending tool, and switch warm-up on.
  8. LinkedIn: connect your account to your LinkedIn outreach tool (the Day 2 guide uses HeyReach). Make your headline state the Step 1 outcome.
  9. WhatsApp: register a number on the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API, not the WhatsApp Business app) through your sending tool or a WhatsApp provider. Then submit your reminder templates today: approval can take days, and reminders go only to people who opted in.
  10. Calendar: connect the calendar you take calls on (Google Calendar or Outlook) and create a discovery-call booking link.
  11. Cloudflare API: My Profile → API Tokens → Create Token, template Edit zone DNS, your outreach domains under Zone Resources. Running Day 3's automations on Cloudflare Workers? Create a second token from Edit Cloudflare Workers.
  12. Store each token in your password manager or your project's .env file, never in a doc or chat. Test it: curl https://api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/user/tokens/verify -H "Authorization: Bearer YOUR_TOKEN" should answer "This API Token is valid and active".

S3: The Nurture Engine (Where Show-Up Is Won)

Here's the number nobody wants to hear: roughly half the people who register never turn up. Not because your content is bad: they haven't seen it yet. Because life happened and nothing reminded them hard enough. The flow below is the fix: in the teaching model it lifts show-up to 89%.

Nurture is the cheapest lift in the entire funnel. You already paid to acquire these people. Every reminder that converts a registration into an attendee costs you almost nothing and is worth exactly as much as a new lead.

The Tantra Pro Flow: six touches before, two branches after
The no-show branch is worth more than it looks. These people registered: they raised their hand and then life got in the way. They are not cold, and treating them like a lost cause is the most common unforced error in the whole funnel.
◆ Do this · send the T−24h reminder at 8am in their timezone

Not 8am in yours. A reminder that lands at 3am is a reminder that gets buried under everything that arrived after it. If your tool can't send by recipient timezone, that's a reason to change tools. This single setting moves show-up more than most copy changes will.

S5: The Close (Offer, Booking, Payment)

A booked call is not revenue. A proposal is not revenue. Money in the account is revenue, and between the offer and the money there are five places people quietly fall out. Build all five now, not after you lose someone at each.

Stage 5 is where attention becomes revenue, and every mechanism feeds the same eleven-stage pipeline: Form Filled → Eval Call Scheduled → Qualified or Unqualified → Sales Call Held → Follow-Up → Proposal Sent → Proposal Accepted → Payment Completed → Onboarding → Delivery, plus a No Show exit off the booked call. Unqualified and No Show recover people instead of losing them. Each stage is entered by an event, not by someone remembering to drag a card, and each one has an owner, a clock and a follow-up that fires on its own. Build it once in GoHighLevel (route A) or in the starter CRM with Claude Code (route B); only the stages in front of Form Filled change with your mechanism.

  1. Ask Claude Code for one variant of the element the table named: a second landing page, or a second version of the Tantra step template.
  2. Split the next run in half. Half the list gets variant A, half gets B, each link carrying its own utm_content, so the dashboard counts them separately.
  3. When the run finishes, read both rows. If the gap is only a handful of people, run the same test again before you call it.
  4. Keep the winner, revert the loser in the spec, and log the change, the date, the metric you expected to move and the result.
  5. Put the weekly review in your calendar as a recurring event.

Example: for a SaaS company, variant B's headline names the integration buyers kept asking about. B wins on registration rate, becomes the page, and next week's experiment moves to show rate.

Step 8: Write the Scale Plan

Task 3.8Write the Scale Plan

Why: Five lines on one page turn the dashboard into next month's decisions. The first two must be fetched numbers, not estimates.

  1. My cost per buyer is ___ and my lifetime value is ___.
  2. My cheapest channel by cost per buyer is ___, so next month's budget moves toward it. Keep a small test budget on every other channel.
  3. My worst step is ___ and this week's experiment is ___.
  4. My ascension offer is ___: the rung above what they just bought.
  5. My retention mechanism is ___: what produces the first win, and by when. Everyone else stays warm through the five tags (webinar registrant, webinar attended, live webinar not watched, watched webinar replay, purchased): each tag triggers its own follow-up.

Example: for a SaaS company, line 3 reads "show rate; this week we test a WhatsApp reminder at one hour before."
